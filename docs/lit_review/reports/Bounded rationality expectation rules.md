# Most bounded-rationality rules already fit hetlearn

You do not have to choose between level-k and adaptive learning: the only published level-k model built for recurrent business-cycle data puts the levels on top of a learner. In Evans, Gibbs and McGough's unified model (*AEJ: Macro* 2025), level-0 forecasts are learned with a constant gain. Level-k forecasts apply the model's temporary-equilibrium map k times to the level-0 forecast each period, and weight moves across levels with forecast performance. The pick-one view holds only in the Farhi-Werning and García-Schmidt-Woodford experiments, where level 0 is a frozen status-quo path. Paul Beaudry's level-k paper (Beaudry, Carter and Lahiri, NBER WP 31741, 2023; IMF WP/26/97, 2026) also starts from an adaptive level 0, "forecast = last observation". Under level k, the seed's effect on inflation is scaled by `(1−θ)^k`, which nests adaptive expectations at k = 0 and RE as k → ∞. In hetlearn's linear structure, level k is the per-quarter solve `F ↦ (A0 − A2F)^{-1}A1` run k times from the learners' PLM. That is anchored RE with the anchor replaced by the law of motion of the level-(k−1) economy. The cost is K matrix inversions per quarter, with no Blanchard-Kahn step. Gabaix's cognitive discounting, in the reduced form hetlearn would estimate, is a multiplier m in front of forecast deviations from steady state. Five conditions apply. The multiplier acts on deviations; it compounds as `m^h` with the horizon; it can differ across households, firms and wage setters; shared expectations such as E_tπ_{t+1} then need equation-specific copies; and it shrinks the model-consistent forecast rather than the anchored one. It combines with learning: Meggiorini and Milani estimate **m ≈ 0.94 under Euler-equation learning against 0.42 under RE**. It also combines mechanically with level-k, although nobody has published that combination. Among the other families, five additions are cheap and high-value: diagnostic expectations with one-period memory (θ = 0.44 in an estimated SW07), SAC/BLE learning (the best SW07 fit found, log10 Bayes factor 9.30 against RE), intercept-only learning with anchoring gains, an anchored sticky- or noisy-information block, and survey-identified expectation wedges. Three structural extensions pay off most: an N-type share vector with multinomial logit, inertia and type costs; a transform layer that can act on any type's forecast; and survey-expectation measurement equations. Without survey data, most of these families are hard to tell apart from habit, indexation, or each other.

Notation follows the toolbox. `x_exp1_t = n1_t·E^RE_t x_{t+1} + n2_t·E^BR_t x_{t+1}` for each learning variable. Anchored RE forecasts are `k + K s_t` on the original model's 25 SW07 states `s_t = T s_{t−1} + R ε_t`; model-consistent forecasts are `x{+1}`; t−1 information uses the chain `x_REt2{-1}` ([FEATURES.md](../../FEATURES.md)). For the linear algebra, write the model as `A0 y_t = A1 y_{t−1} + A2 Ê_t y_{t+1} + B ε_t`, where `Ê` is the population-average subjective expectation and enters only for the selected variables. Formulas marked "derived" come from the research notes behind this report, not from a published source, and should be checked numerically before use.

## Level-k iterates the model's own map on the learners' forecasts

### Beaudry, Carter and Lahiri seed level-k with adaptive expectations

The Beaudry paper is Paul Beaudry, Thomas J. Carter and Amartya Lahiri, "The Central Bank's Dilemma: Look Through Supply Shocks or Control Inflation Expectations?", **NBER Working Paper 31741 (September 2023)** ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31741)). It also appeared as **IMF Working Paper WP/26/97 (May 2026)** ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/wp/2026/english/wpiea2026097-source-pdf.pdf)). An earlier version circulated as Bank of Canada Staff Working Paper 2022-41 under the title "Looking through supply shocks versus controlling inflation expectations: understanding the central bank dilemma" ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/bcabocawp/22-41.htm)). I found no journal version. The model plays down intertemporal coordination on purpose. Wages are set one period ahead and prices are flexible, so the Phillips curve is `π̂_t = E^WS_{t−1}N̂_t + E^WS_{t−1}π̂_t − ε̂_t`, where ε̂ is a productivity surprise. The central bank announces the stance θ_t of a feedback rule `N̂_t = −θ_t π̂_t` before the shock. Each wage setter must forecast the others' forecasts, which turns each period into "a static coordination game to which LKT can readily be applied" ([NBER WP 31741](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31741/w31741.pdf)).

Level 0 is adaptive: `E^0_{t−1}π̂_t = π̂_{t−1}` and `E^0_{t−1}N̂_t = N̂_{t−1}`. Level k pushes the seed through the Phillips curve and the announced rule, which gives `E^k_{t−1}π̂_t = (1−θ_t)^{k−1}[E^0 N̂_t + E^0 π̂_t]`. The equilibrium is `π̂_t = (1−θ_t)^k(N̂_{t−1} + π̂_{t−1}) − ε̂_t`. **It equals adaptive expectations at k = 0 and collapses to the RE outcome as k → ∞, provided θ_t ∈ (0, 1].** The baseline gives every wage setter the same k, so expectations are common and possibly wrong. The law of iterated expectations fails "because each wage-setter believes that they are more sophisticated than all other wage-setters". The calibration is annual and Canadian (κ = 0.092, β_CB = 0.995), with **k = 2**, "in the middle of the range of k = 1 to 4 considered in Farhi and Werning (2019)". The optimal policy looks through supply shocks until an overheating state `x_{t−1} = π̂ + N̂` reaches about **4%**, then jumps to a hawkish stance (a "pivot"). Thresholds lie at 3–5% for k ≤ 3; with k = 40 there are no pivots even at 10% ([NBER WP 31741](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31741/w31741.pdf)). Appendix H of the 2026 version adds a reflective variant, `E_{t−1}x_t = exp(−kθ_t)x_{t−1}`, and a cognitive-hierarchy variant with k+1 levels, and the pivots survive in both. Its empirical illustration is reduced form. Residuals of US one-year expectations (Michigan and Cleveland Fed), regressed on lagged trimmed inflation and V/U, rise from about 2020Q2 until the 2022Q2 tightening and then revert ([IMF WP/26/97](https://imf.org/-/media/files/publications/wp/2026/english/wpiea2026097-source-pdf.pdf)).

Two features matter for hetlearn. First, BCL apply level-k to a t−1 nowcast of period-t outcomes, not to `E_t x_{t+1}` in Euler equations. Their mechanism comes from the announced θ_t entering every level-≥1 forecast while level 0 ignores it. A toolbox version therefore needs the same split: a policy change, as in applications 3 and 4, enters level-≥1 beliefs but not the level-0 PLM. Second, `(1−θ)^k → 0` requires θ ∈ (0, 2). For θ ∈ (1, 2) the interaction turns into strategic substitutes and the levels alternate in sign. Angeletos and Lian call this oscillation "a bug, not a feature" ([NBER WP 29776](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29776/w29776.pdf)).

### Level 0, the level index and the shares differ across papers

The literature agrees on the recursion, level k = best response to level k−1. It differs on everything around it. The table collects the specifications. **Indexing is not uniform.** Farhi-Werning, Bianchi-Vimercati-Eichenbaum-Guerreiro and Iovino-Sergeyev call the status-quo believers "level 1". BCL, EGM and Qiu call the naive rule level 0, as hetlearn would.

The split by use case is clean. **A frozen status-quo level 0 is built for one-off announcements**: pegs, forward guidance, QE, fiscal stimulus at the ZLB. Farhi and Werning say they study "relatively unfamiliar scenarios ... where learning protocols that converge to rational expectations cannot be naturally invoked" ([FW NBER WP 23281](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23281/w23281.pdf)). **A learned or backward-looking level 0 is built for recurrent business-cycle data**, which is where hetlearn and SW07 live. The level-0 choice also matters numerically. Angeletos and Lian call it "a 'free variable'". When the GE feedback lies outside (−1, 1), "the sensitivity of the level-k outcome to ... the arbitrarily chosen level-0 outcome explodes" ([NBER WP 29776](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29776/w29776.pdf)).

### In a linear model, level k is the per-quarter solve run k times

In every paper, level k is a T-map applied k times to the level-0 belief. T maps a belief to the outcome of an economy where every agent holds that belief. EGM write it as `E^k_{t−1}y_t = T^k(a)` with `T(a) = μ + βa` in the univariate case, and say level-k deductions "expand to include the forward guidance policy and to the multivariate case in the same way" ([EGM](https://pages.uoregon.edu/gevans/EGM_Unified_Model_AEJM_Final_Dec2023.pdf)). Qiu's recursive version is the construction hetlearn needs: "Perceiving others as one level below is equivalent to using the actual equilibrium objects of this level as perceived equilibrium objects" ([Qiu](https://economics.sas.upenn.edu/system/files/2018-03/Zhesheng%20(JMP%2001-30).pdf)). In matrix form (derived), level 0 is a PLM forecast embedded in the full state, `E^0_t y_{t+1} = a_0 + F_0 y_t`, either held fixed or RLS-updated. With period-t information, the level-j economy solves `y_t = M_j A2 a_j + M_j A1 y_{t−1} + M_j B ε_t` with `M_j = (A0 − A2F_j)^{-1}`. Level j+1 forecasts with that law of motion:

`F_{j+1} = (A0 − A2F_j)^{-1}A1,    a_{j+1} = (A0 − A2F_j)^{-1}A2 a_j`

This is the brute-force iteration for the MSV solution `F = (A0 − A2F)^{-1}A1`, stopped after k steps and started from the PLM. With t−1 information there is no simultaneity: `H_j = A0^{-1}(A1 + A2F_j)` and `c_j = A0^{-1}A2 a_j`, and the two-step forecast gives `F_{j+1} = H_j²` and `a_{j+1} = (I + H_j)c_j`. With fixed Nagel-type shares ω_0..ω_K, `Ê_t y_{t+1} = Σ_k ω_k(a_k + F_k y_t)`. The realized law of motion is `y_t = (A0 − A2Σω_kF_k)^{-1}(A1 y_{t−1} + A2Σω_k a_k + B ε_t)`, which generalizes `n1·E^RE + n2·E^BR` from two anchors to K+1. Cognitive hierarchy replaces the lower-level economy by the normalized mixture of all lower levels: `F_k = (A0 − A2Σ_{h<k}g_k(h)F_h)^{-1}A1`, with `g_k(h) = ω_h/Σ_{l<k}ω_l`, and Poisson shares `ω_h = e^{−τ}τ^h/h!` truncated at K. A damped (reflective) step, `F^{(j+1)} = F^{(j)} + λ[T(F^{(j)}) − F^{(j)}]`, approximates the GSW ODE when λ is small and the number of steps is large. Two properties matter. **First, if level 0 is the current RE solution, every level coincides with it**, because T(F*) = F*. Level-k only does something when level 0 differs from RE: a learned PLM, the steady state, an old-regime solution, or beliefs that ignore an announced change. Second, levels ≥ 1 forecast with the full state vector, shocks included, even when level 0 is a univariate AR(1). They carry the same strong information assumption as anchored RE agents.

**Convergence is stricter than the E-stability the learners already need.** At the RE fixed point the Jacobian of T is (derived) `vec(dT) = (F*′ ⊗ M A2)vec(dF)`, with eigenvalues λ_i(F*)·μ_j(MA2). Discrete level-k converges only if every product has modulus below one, which is iterative E-stability. The damped or continuous version needs only `Re(λ_iμ_j) < 1`, the ordinary E-stability test. An SW07 parameterization can therefore be E-stable, so hetlearn's learners converge, and still fail IE-stability, so that high-level forecasts oscillate or explode. The published results line up with this. GSW show that discrete reflection can "fail to converge as k is made large, owing to the existence of a negative eigenvalue of absolute value greater than one". In their NK model the Taylor principle is necessary and sufficient for the continuous process to converge, and beliefs diverge under a permanent peg ([GSW](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21614/w21614.pdf)). Farhi and Werning find each level-k equilibrium "uniquely determined" even under flexible prices, with convergence that depends on the policy rule; level-k thus "sidesteps issues of indeterminacy" ([FW](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23281/w23281.pdf)). Qiu reports that the recursive level-k equilibrium fails to converge in an RBC model and adds incomplete markets to restore it ([Qiu](https://economics.sas.upenn.edu/system/files/2018-03/Zhesheng%20(JMP%2001-30).pdf)). Gibbs and McClung (2023), "Does my model predict a forward guidance puzzle?", *RED* 51: 393–423, show that bounded forward-guidance responses follow from conditions that are a special case of E-stability and "distinct from determinacy" ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/zbw/bofrdp/rdp019.html)). I found no published IE-stability check for an estimated medium-scale model. The practical rules are to compute the spectral radius of the level-k Jacobian at each draw, cap K at 1–4 (the range the literature uses), and offer the damped variant. Because each finite level is unique, hetlearn could also simulate SW07 under pegs or passive rules with level-k agents, but only when no model-consistent RE type is present.

### Learning and level-k are complements, and the lab says both are needed

The belief that one must choose between the two comes from the Farhi-Werning setup, and Farhi and Werning say so themselves: "Modeling how level-k agents react when they see unexpected realizations would require some hybrid of level-k reasoning and learning that is beyond the scope of the current paper". EGM present their model as that hybrid. "Level-0 forecasts evolve over time via adaptive learning. Agents revise over time their depth of reasoning in response to forecast errors, observed and counterfactual." They separate "adaptive dynamics" (learned level 0, fixed weights), "replicator dynamics" (fixed level 0, moving weights) and "unified dynamics" (both). They also frame the Brock-Hommes predictor menu as "the full set of level-k forecasts" ([EGM](https://pages.uoregon.edu/gevans/EGM_Unified_Model_AEJM_Final_Dec2023.pdf)). In three learning-to-forecast treatments the unified model's price MSE relative to RE is **0.45, 0.93 and 0.53**. The comparison models score 1.69, 1.22 and 1.36 (adaptive learning alone), 0.94, 8.16 and 0.56 (fixed level-k) and 0.74, 1.37 and 1.33 (replicator only). The authors conclude that "each of underlying mechanisms – adaptive learning, level-k and replicator dynamics – are needed". Anufriev, Duffy and Panchenko (2022, *JET* 201: 105417) reach the same verdict on their own data ([EGM](https://pages.uoregon.edu/gevans/EGM_Unified_Model_AEJM_Final_Dec2023.pdf)). In EGM's NK forward-guidance simulation (gain 0.2), more than 30% of forecasts still come from levels 0–3 by period 10, and low-level reasoning weakens forward guidance.

The real design choice is therefore between two models, plus a choice of share rule. A **frozen level 0** (the steady state, or the pre-announcement RE solution) suits one-off announcement exercises. A **learned level 0** (hetlearn's PLM learners) suits estimation, and the shares can then be fixed or switch on performance. Two warnings apply. EGM show that with a fixed level 0 and feedback below one in modulus, replicator switching moves weight toward ever higher levels, i.e. toward RE, and a learned level 0 converges toward the REE as well. Low levels persist only with repeated structural change or noise. Iovino and Sergeyev find the same unraveling when the starting point learns: asset purchases must grow to keep a constant effect ([IS 2019 draft](https://www.suomenpankki.fi/globalassets/bof/en/research/seminars-and-conferences/conferences-and-workshops/documents/cepr2020/iovino_luigi_12518_41615_paper_1.pdf)). EGM's footnote 7 states the timing convention hetlearn would adopt: changes "known at time t − 1 that occurs in time t are built directly into the forecasts of agents for which k ≥ 1", while level 0 is not state-contingent.

### One Bayesian estimate exists, and it is small-scale and unpublished

**Qiu (2018), "Level-k DSGE and Monetary Policy", is the only Bayesian level-k DSGE estimate I found.** It uses US GDP growth, GDP-deflator inflation, the funds rate and Michigan one-year growth and inflation expectations (with measurement error on the two survey series), and a "Uniform 1/k" prior on [1, ∞). The posterior mean is **k = 1.265, 95% band [1.197, 1.327]**, "close to the results in experimental games". Identification comes from "the missing co-movements for indirect channels", which pin k in [1, 2], with the weakened direct channels giving the exact value ([Qiu](https://economics.sas.upenn.edu/system/files/2018-03/Zhesheng%20(JMP%2001-30).pdf)). His 2024 CV still lists it as a working paper ([CV](https://econ.hkust.edu.hk/sites/economics/files/faculty/cv_zheshqiu_2024.pdf)). Iovino and Sergeyev combine narratively identified GSE mortgage purchases with Blue Chip forecast errors and back out **86% "level-1" thinkers**, those who do not revise after the intervention ([IS 2019 draft](https://www.suomenpankki.fi/globalassets/bof/en/research/seminars-and-conferences/conferences-and-workshops/documents/cepr2020/iovino_luigi_12518_41615_paper_1.pdf)). EGM classify 47.3% of lab forecasts as level 0–3 or REE in the first announcement round, and 64.4–66.0% later. Everything else is calibrated. **An estimated SW07 with level-k or cognitive-hierarchy expectations, jointly with gains and switching parameters, is an open gap.** Integer k is awkward for MCMC. The usable continuous embeddings are Poisson τ, Qiu's linear interpolation between adjacent levels ("a level-1.3 agent perceiving 30% of the others as level-1"), BEG's λ, and BCL's exponent.

## Cognitive discounting is a multiplier on deviations, under five conditions

### Gabaix's operator compounds with the horizon and relaxes the Taylor principle

The reference is Gabaix, X. (2020), "A Behavioral New Keynesian Model", *American Economic Review* 110(8): 2271–2327, NBER WP 22954 ([AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20162005); volume and pages from the [Meggiorini-Milani reference list](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp9039.pdf)). It builds on Gabaix (2014), "A Sparsity-Based Model of Bounded Rationality", *QJE* 129(4): 1661–1710, and is surveyed in Gabaix (2019), "Behavioral Inattention", *Handbook of Behavioral Economics* vol. 2: 261–344 (same reference list). The agent perceives the de-meaned state as `X_{t+1} = m(ΓX_t + ε_{t+1})`, m ∈ [0, 1]. Lemma 2.2 then gives **`E^BR_t[z(X_{t+k})] = m^k E_t[z(X_{t+k})]` for any variable with z(0) = 0**, endogenous variables included. With a non-zero mean the rule applies to deviations, `E^BR_t[X_{t+k} − X*] = m^k E_t[X_{t+k} − X*]`, and the agent stays rational about the average real rate and the balanced growth path ([Gabaix 2019 manuscript](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)). The IS curve becomes `x_t = M E_t x_{t+1} − σ(i_t − E_tπ_{t+1} − r^n_t)` with M = m. Solved forward, `x_t = −σΣ_k M^k E_t[r̂_{t+k} − r̂^n_{t+k}]`, so a rate change k quarters ahead carries weight M^k and the forward-guidance puzzle disappears. The Phillips curve is `π_t = βM^f E_tπ_{t+1} + κx_t` with **`M^f = m[θ + (1−βθ)(1−θ)/(1−βθm)] < m`**, because a resetting firm discounts cumulative inflation since its reset date. The calibration is M = 0.85 and M^f = 0.80, an attention half-life of about a year. Determinacy requires `φ_π + ((1−βM^f)/κ)φ_x + (1−βM^f)(1−M)/(κσ) > 1`. An interest-rate peg is therefore determinate when `(1−βM^f)(1−M)/(κσ) > 1`, which at the calibration means M < 0.86. Two remarks in Gabaix bear directly on estimation. His footnote 4 notes that CD agents' "mental model never converges to the rational expectations solution", unlike learners. His footnote 26 notes that M and the shock variance are not separately identified from the output-gap series in a simple specification ([Gabaix 2019 manuscript](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)).

Every US study I found prefers CD to RE. The estimate falls when CD is the only departure from RE and rises toward one once learning carries part of the load. The firm parameter is lower than the household one whenever the two are estimated separately, and it is weakly identified: the NKPC robust set in Andrade et al. spans [0.07, 0.95]. Tight priors stay close to their means (Hirose et al.; Erceg, Jakab and Lindé's euro-area m = 0.94 under a Beta(0.975, 0.0125) prior, as reported by [Meggiorini](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/18r6t6qb)). Meggiorini adds that the importance of CD "is (expectedly) reduced in a larger model with as many frictions as the Smets and Wouters model". **For hetlearn, the natural prior target is m ≈ 0.9–0.95 when CD sits on top of learners and 0.6–0.75 when RE agents are the only alternative.**

### The multiplier is right for the reduced form, under five conditions

Your description, "a discounting term in front of the forward-looking terms", is what Meggiorini proves for linearized models. "Given a linearized DSGE model under rational expectations, its behavioral counterpart can be derived by introducing the discounting parameter multiplicatively in front of all expectational variables." Her behavioral SW07 equations show what this means in practice: `c_t = c_1c_{t−1} + m(1−c_1)E_tc_{t+1} + c_2(l_t − mE_tl_{t+1}) − c_3(r_t − mE_tπ_{t+1}) + ε^b_t` and `q_t = mq_1E_tq_{t+1} + m(1−q_1)E_tr^k_{t+1} − (r_t − mE_tπ_{t+1}) + ε^b_t`, with the non-expectational equations unchanged. Erceg, Jakab and Lindé's ad hoc multiplier on all forward-looking variables is "ex-post equivalent" ([Meggiorini dissertation](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/18r6t6qb)). Five conditions decide whether a toolbox multiplier reproduces Gabaix.

**The multiplier acts on deviations.** The forecast is `x̄ + m(E_t x_{t+1} − x̄)`. In demeaned SW07 this is `m·E_t x_{t+1}`, but learners need a stated reference point: the true steady state (Gabaix's default), the PLM's perceived mean `(I − φ_1)^{-1}φ_0` (in the spirit of Gabaix's Section 6.3 "default inflation"), or the last observation. Meggiorini and Milani multiply the whole PLM forecast, intercept included, which amounts to the first choice with demeaned data ([Meggiorini-Milani](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp9039.pdf)).

**It compounds with the horizon.** The literal operator puts `m^h` on a forecast h periods past the information date. A t−1-dated forecast of x_{t+1} would carry m², although Meggiorini and Milani use a single M in their t−1 Euler-equation system. At m = 0.9 the difference between 0.81 and 0.9 is not negligible, so the choice should be an option.

**Agents can differ, and the Phillips-curve coefficient is disputed.** Gabaix's footnote 15 allows a separate firm parameter m^f. Benchimol and Bounader (*JFS* 67, 2023: 101151) and Meggiorini argue that Gabaix applies the horizon discount to the level rather than the deviation in the pricing problem, and they derive `π_t = βmE_tπ_{t+1} + κy_t` instead of βM^f ([HAL](https://hal-emse.ccsd.cnrs.fr/emse-04624979); [Meggiorini](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/18r6t6qb)). If hetlearn estimates the coefficient b on expected inflation, both readings are nested: `m^f = b/β`, or the solution of `b/β = m^f[θ + (1−θ)(1−βθ)/(1−βθm^f)]`. SW07's Phillips curve with indexation would need its own derivation under the Gabaix reading, while the Meggiorini reading has a published medium-scale estimate.

**Shared expectations need equation-specific copies.** E_tπ_{t+1} enters the consumption Euler equation, the Q equation, the price Phillips curve and the wage equation. Separate m^h, m^f and m^w therefore require expectation auxiliaries per equation (for example `pi_exp_hh`, `pi_exp_firm`), not one per variable. Whether expected inflation in the real rate is discounted at all also needs a switch. Gabaix's baseline leaves it undiscounted unless consumer attention to inflation is added (his footnote 34), finite planning horizons discount it with the same ρ (λ = ρ in GHL's notation), and Meggiorini discounts it ([Gabaix](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf); [GHL](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2020003pap.pdf)).

**The multiplier shrinks the model-consistent forecast.** Gabaix's E_t is the rational expectation of the behavioral economy itself. In hetlearn that is the `'model_consistent'` RE forecast, re-solved each quarter, and the solver must check Blanchard-Kahn conditions with the multipliers in place. Because CD enlarges the determinacy region, the priors on policy coefficients and m interact. Shrinking anchored forecasts instead gives "CD about the RE economy", which is a different, partly backward-looking model.

A population mix adds an identification problem. With fixed shares, `n1 + n2·m` is a single effective multiplier, so n2 and m are not separately identified. With switching on squared errors, the effective multiplier `n1_t + n2_t·m` moves over time, which can identify both. But if the RE type is model-consistent, the CD forecast has weakly larger expected squared error than the true conditional mean, and switching drives CD agents out unless the RE type pays a cost or the CD type gets a predisposition term. Against anchored RE or AR(1) learners, CD can win. I found no published heterogeneous-shares model with a CD type, so this has no empirical precedent.

### CD stacks on learners in published work and on level-k in principle

Meggiorini and Milani estimate the combination: "The two behavioral elements can, in principle, coexist". Expectations are formed from the PLM and then discounted, `E^BR_t Y_{t+k} = M^k E_t Y_{t+k}` with E_t subjective. Under Euler-equation learning they find **m = 0.94 [0.89, 0.98] with gain 0.0049 [0.002, 0.01]**, a better fit than both RE and IH learning. Under infinite-horizon learning, m falls to 0.20 because IH learners are "remarkably farsighted" and CD must offset that ([Meggiorini-Milani](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp9039.pdf)). Split samples give m = 0.94 before 1979 and 0.79 after 1982 under EE learning. With variable-specific gains (0.0059 output, 0.0370 inflation, 0.0198 rate), m = 0.88 and the log ML improves to −266.27. This is hetlearn's architecture with a single learner type, which makes it the natural validation target.

No paper combines CD with level-k. There are two natural designs: shrink each level's forecast of the next-period aggregate while iterating the T-map, or discount only the level-0 anchor. Both multiply dampening factors, so separating k from m on macro data will be weak. Angeletos and Lian name the discriminating moment, the horizon profile of under-reaction. CD gives `m^k E_t[c_{t+k}]`, which grows with the horizon. Shallow reasoning gives `λE_t[c_{t+k}]`, flat in k. Incomplete information with learning gives `μ_kE_t[c_{t+k}]` with μ_k rising in k, "opposite to" CD ([NBER WP 29776](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29776/w29776.pdf)). With one-step Euler-equation expectations this profile barely shows. Multi-horizon survey forecasts are therefore what separates the family members. In Euler-equation form, shallow reasoning (Angeletos and Sastry) is the same object as one-step CD.

The Angeletos-Huo reduced form is the closest cousin with a different shape. In Angeletos, G.-M. and Z. Huo (2021), "Myopia and Anchoring", *AER* 111(4) ([AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20191436)), dispersed noisy information is observationally equivalent to `a_t = ϕξ_t + δω_fE_t[a_{t+1}] + ω_b a_{t−1}` with ω_f < 1 and ω_b > 0. Disciplined by the Coibion-Gorodnichenko SPF coefficient (1.193, s.d. 0.185), the NKPC weights are **(ω_f, ω_b) = (0.52, 0.43)**, close to Galí-Gertler-López-Salido's hybrid estimates (0.618, 0.374) ([NBER WP 24545](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24545/w24545.pdf)). Level-k and Angeletos-Lian models "help generate ω_f < 1 but restrict ω_b = 0", because their beliefs never learn. The weights are not policy-invariant: they rise with noise and with GE feedback. In SW07 the ω_b term also competes with habit and indexation.

A caution applies to the whole dampening family. Angeletos, Huo and Sastry find that SPF unemployment and inflation forecasts under-react on impact and overshoot at medium horizons after the main business-cycle shock. That pattern gives "little support for theories emphasizing under-extrapolation or two close cousins of it, cognitive discounting and level-K thinking" and favours dispersed noisy information plus over-extrapolation ([NBER WP 27308](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27308/w27308.pdf); *NBER Macroeconomics Annual 2020*, vol. 35, [chapter page](https://www.nber.org/books-and-chapters/nber-macroeconomics-annual-2020-volume-35/imperfect-macroeconomic-expectations-evidence-and-theory)). CD and level-k remain good devices for damping GE propagation in the model. As descriptions of measured expectations they need survey support before they are stacked on information frictions.

## The back-pocket families reduce to six reusable components

Almost every remaining family enters hetlearn through one of six components. The first is a transform of an existing forecast: a multiplier, the diagnostic difference, or partial adjustment. The second is an anchored backward-looking state block built from the original model's (T, K). The third is a new PLM, updating rule or gain rule in the learner's slot. The fourth is a BR type with fixed or equilibrium beliefs, and the fifth is a third or later type. The sixth is an additive wedge with its own exogenous process. All six keep the model linear and Gaussian given the past, so the time-varying Kalman filter and Bayesian estimation carry over unchanged. The hard cases are the ones that need agents' value functions, higher-order beliefs about endogenous variables, or infinite-horizon decision rules derived by hand. The tables in this section give, for each family, the linear expectation term, the US evidence and the mapping. Difficulty means: easy = a new function in an existing slot; medium = a new state block or a fixed point inside the likelihood; hard = a model rewrite or a new equilibrium concept.

### Sticky, epidemiological and noisy information collapse to one anchored block

Sticky information averages current and past full-information forecasts, `F_t x_{t+1} = λΣ_j(1−λ)^jE_{t−j}x_{t+1}`, and returns RE at λ = 1. The reference is Mankiw, N. G. and R. Reis (2007), "Sticky Information in General Equilibrium", *JEEA* 5(2–3): 603–613 ([Mankiw](https://mankiw.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/sticky-information-general-equilibrium)). The exact recursion `F_t x_{t+1} = λE_t x_{t+1} + (1−λ)F_{t−1}x_{t+1}` keeps lengthening the forecast horizon, so in general equilibrium the state is infinite. Mankiw and Reis estimate on US data 1954–2006 that **consumers and workers update every 5–6 quarters and firms every 1.5 quarters** (Bayesian), and conclude that stickiness is "pervasive" ([extended version](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/reisr/papers/07-SIGE-long.pdf)). The SI Phillips curve alone fares worse. Coibion (2010, *REStat* 92(1): 87–101) finds it statistically dominated by the NKPC ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/restat/v92y2010i1p87-101.html)). Dupor, Kitamura and Tsuruga (2010, *REStat* 92(3): 657–669) find that a dual-stickiness model fits about as well as the hybrid NKPC ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/restat/v92y2010i3p657-669.html)). Truncation is expensive. Verona and Wolters (*Computational Economics* 43, 2014: 357–370) need **571 equations at 16 lags and 1,795 at 32**, and judge Dynare estimation "unfeasible" ([BoF](https://publications.bof.fi/handle/10024/47608); [WP](https://www.kielinstitut.de/fileadmin/Dateiverwaltung/IfW-Publications/fis-import/c1268090-6443-4541-bd17-a758caa0ed32-VeronaWolters.pdf)). Under anchored forecasts the problem disappears (derived). Each past forecast is `k + KT^js_{t−j}`, so `E^SI_t x_{t+1} = k + KW_t` with **`W_t = λs_t + (1−λ)TW_{t−1}`**, or `V_t = λTs_{t−1} + (1−λ)TV_{t−1}` with the Mankiw-Reis information lag. One block of 25 SW07 states serves every SI variable, with no truncation. The model-consistent version extends hetlearn's E_{t−1} chain to about J(J+1)/2 auxiliaries per variable, roughly 150 at J = 16, and would be slow inside the per-quarter re-solve.

Carroll's epidemiological model is the one-lag special case for households who absorb the professional forecast with probability λ: `M_t = λS_t + (1−λ)M_{t−1}`. On Michigan against SPF inflation expectations, 1981q3–2000q2, **λ = 0.27**, and λ = 0.25 is "easily accepted" (Carroll, NBER WP 8695, published in *QJE* 2003) ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w8695)). In hetlearn this is a type whose forecast is `λ(k + Ks_t) + (1−λ)x_EPI_{t−1}`: adaptive expectations toward the RE forecast rather than toward data. It takes one lag per variable.

Noisy information gives the same consensus form with a Kalman gain, `F_t x_{t+h} = GE_t x_{t+h} + (1−G)F_{t−1}x_{t+h}`, so the Coibion-Gorodnichenko coefficient is β = (1−G)/G ([NBER WP 16537](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16537)). SPF inflation 1969–2010 gives **β = 1.23 (0.50), i.e. G ≈ 0.45**: forecasters put less than half the weight on new information. The friction depends on the sample. Angeletos, Huo and Sastry report a consensus coefficient for inflation of 1.528 (0.418) over 1968–2017 but 0.292 (0.191) over 1984–2017. Individual forecasts over-react after 1984 (−0.263, s.e. 0.054) ([NBER WP 27308](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27308/w27308.pdf)). The anchored version is the SI block with λ replaced by G: `Ŝ_t = Gs_t + (1−G)TŜ_{t−1}` and `E^NI_t x_{t+1} = k + KŜ_t`. A matrix gain comes from one Riccati solve per parameter draw, because the anchored (T, R) does not move with beliefs or shares (derived). The model-consistent version needs higher-order beliefs about endogenous variables. Melosi (2017), "Signalling Effects of Monetary Policy", *REStud* 84(2): 853–884, truncates the belief hierarchy and estimates it on seven observables including SPF inflation expectations, 1970Q3–2007Q4. The **log marginal likelihood is −319.89 against −334.95** for perfect information, with the caveat that the perfect-information model has one more shock ([Chicago Fed WP](https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/working-papers/2016/wp2016-14-pdf.pdf?sc_lang=en); [SUFE listing](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/343988)). That route is hard in hetlearn, because the hierarchy's fixed point would change every quarter.

Rational inattention makes the signal structure, and hence G, an optimal choice. Maćkowiak and Wiederholt solve it by iterating between attention choices and aggregate laws of motion, and calibrate rather than estimate ([ECB WP 1331](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecbwp1331.pdf)). Afrouzi and Yang supply fast LQG dynamic-RI solvers with a MATLAB repository, DRIPs.m ([CESifo WP 8840](https://cesifo.org/en/publications/2021/working-paper/dynamic-rational-inattention-and-phillips-curve); [DRIPs](https://docs.juliahub.com/General/DRIPs/stable/syntax/)). I found no likelihood-estimated RI DSGE on US data. A medium-cost anchored version solves the RI problem once per draw on the fixed (T, R) and feeds the gain into the noisy-information block. A cheaper stand-in ties G to recent error volatility through the gain-rule slot, matching Coibion and Gorodnichenko's finding that rigidity falls in volatile periods and after recessions start.

### Diagnostic expectations cost almost nothing; natural expectations already exist

The diagnostic operator is **`E^θ_t x_{t+1} = E_t x_{t+1} + θ(E_t x_{t+1} − E_{t−1}x_{t+1})`**. L'Huillier, Singh and Yoo (2024), "Incorporating Diagnostic Expectations into the New Keynesian Framework", *REStud* 91(5): 3013–3046, show the model has a unique RE representation with lagged expectations, solved by standard methods ([REStud](https://restud.com/incorporating-diagnostic-expectations-into-the-new-keynesian-framework); [FRBSF WP 2023-19](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/wp2023-19.pdf)). Their estimates on US data include **θ = 0.44 (90% band 0.18–0.69) in Smets-Wouters with Rotemberg pricing**, log ML −897.91 against −900.69 under RE; 0.43 in Justiniano-Primiceri-Tambalotti; and **0.73 (0.59–0.87) in a BLL-type model with SPF forecasts as observables**, log ML −1812.71 against −1847.38. Bianchi, Ilut and Saijo (2024), "Diagnostic Business Cycles", *REStud* 91(1): 129–162, allow distant memory, `E^r_t = Σ_jα_jE_{t−j}`. Their agents project with the shadow RE law of motion from states inherited from the DE economy, which is the anchored idea. Matching impulse responses to Romer-Romer shocks gives **θ = 1.97 with reference weights centred six quarters back**, a log ML 24 points above RE, and θ = 0 if one-period memory is imposed ([Duke](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1488037); [accepted manuscript](https://www.restud.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/MS30913manuscript.pdf)). Bordalo, Gennaioli, Ma and Shleifer find θ > 0 for 17 of 20 SPF series, about 0.6 on average and 0.45 for the GDP deflator ([NBER WP 24932](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24932/w24932.pdf)).

Both RE variants already contain the ingredients. The anchored version with one-period memory is `(1+θ)(k + Ks_t) − θ(k + KTs_{t−1})`. The model-consistent version is `(1+θ)x_REt1 − θx_REt2{-1}`, with extra `θ(x_t − x_REt1{-1})` terms wherever the microfounded equation puts current variables inside the operator, as LSY require. Because `x_REt2{-1}` was computed under last quarter's solution, the reference forecast stays real-time under quarterly re-solving. Two warnings apply (derived). The law of iterated expectations survives only if the operator acts on whole linear combinations including date-t terms, and LSY re-derived SW07 with Rotemberg pricing, so a Calvo version needs its own check. Large θ can also push draws toward explosive regions, where the projection facility will bind. Distant memory with anchoring can be built from cascaded geometric filters: `W1_t = λs_t + (1−λ)TW1_{t−1}` and `W2_t = λW1_t + (1−λ)TW2_{t−1}` give a hump-shaped kernel `λ²(j+1)(1−λ)^j` peaking near (1−λ)/λ, about six quarters at λ ≈ 1/7, at 25 states per stage. Applied to learners, DE is `(1+θ)F^PLM_t − θF^PLM_{t−1}`, using the PLM's two-step forecast stored from the previous quarter. Applied on top of anchored noisy information, `k + K[(1+θ)Ŝ_t − θTŜ_{t−1}]` reproduces the BGMS mechanism: consensus under-reaction combined with individual over-reaction. This is the pair Angeletos, Huo and Sastry favour. **The identification warning is sharp: without survey data the SW07 gain is 2.78 log points; with SPF data in the BLL model it is 34.67.**

Natural expectations are a restricted PLM: low-order AR(p) rules in growth rates, which overstate persistence when the truth is hump-shaped. The references are Fuster, Laibson and Mendel (2010), *JEP* 24(4): 67–84 ([AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.24.4.67)), and Fuster, Hebert and Laibson, NBER WP 17301, in *NBER Macroeconomics Annual 2011* ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17301)). hetlearn already covers them with an AR(p) PLM on derived growth regressors, and the FLM weight on RE is the population share. The survey evidence is unfavourable. Gorodnichenko's discussion scores natural expectations as failing most information-rigidity facts, since they produce no disagreement and no learning ([slides](https://www.frbsf.org/05-Gorodnichenko-discussion.pdf)). BGMS find that beliefs exaggerate long-run reversals rather than miss them. NE and DE make opposite long-horizon predictions after hump-shaped shocks, which the SPF horizon structure can test.

### Finite planning horizons beat RE mostly through their learned trends

In Woodford's finite-horizon planning, agents make state-contingent plans k periods ahead and value the end of the plan with a coarse continuation value learned from data. The reference is Woodford (2019), "Monetary Policy Analysis When Planning Horizons Are Finite", *NBER Macroeconomics Annual 2018* 33(1): 1–50 (per the [Meggiorini-Milani reference list](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp9039.pdf)). The estimated version is Gust, Herbst and López-Salido (2022), "Short-Term Planning, Monetary Policy, and Macroeconomic Persistence", *AEJ: Macroeconomics* 14(4): 174–209 ([AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/mac.20200058); replication files [ICPSR](https://www.openicpsr.org/openicpsr/project/130329/view)). With horizon shares `ω_j = (1−ρ)ρ^j`, the aggregate model is an NK model in deviations from endogenous trends: `y_t − ξ_t − ȳ_t = ρE_t[y_{t+1} − ξ_{t+1} − ȳ_{t+1}] − σ[i_t − ī_t − ρE_t(π_{t+1} − π̄_{t+1})]` and `π_t − π̄_t = βρE_t[π_{t+1} − π̄_{t+1}] + κ(y_t − y*_t − ȳ_t)`. ρ multiplies every forward term, expected inflation in the real rate included. The trends come from constant-gain updates of value-function intercepts, `v_{t+1} = (1−γ)v_t + γv^est_t`, and form a backward-looking block, `x̄_t = (1−ρ)ΘV_t` with `V_t = (I−Γ)V_{t−1} + ΓΦx_{t−1}`. RE returns as ρ → 1. The cyclical block obeys a modified Taylor principle, `((1−ρβ)/κ)φ_y + φ_π > ρ` ([FEDS 2020-003](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2020003pap.pdf)).

On US data 1966Q1–2007Q4, GHL estimate **ρ = 0.46–0.69 and household gains of 0.06–0.14** (a firm gain of 0.31 when estimated separately). "About 50 percent of households and firms have planning horizons that include only the current quarter". The FHP variants reach log MDD −714.59 to −725.69 against −753.63 for the RE NK model, and a representative one-quarter horizon does best at −710.57. The RE model with exogenous AR(1) trends reaches −712.52, within two log points of the best FHP. **Most of FHP's gain over RE therefore comes from slow-moving trends, which FHP generates endogenously through learned continuation values.** Its trend inflation tracks SPF ten-year expectations, and supply-shock persistence falls from near one to about 0.5 ([FEDS 2020-003](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2020003pap.pdf)). Meggiorini's three-equation comparison also ranks FHP first among behavioral models: ρ = 0.69 with gain 0.49, log ML −807.42 ([dissertation](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/18r6t6qb)). Dupraz and Marx describe FHP with long-term learning as a generalization of "both cognitive discounting models and learning models"; their optimal policy "does not display brusque reversals resembling a pivot" ([Banque de France draft](https://www.clevelandfed.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/clevelandfedsite/events/inflation-dynamics-and-drivers/2024/dupraz_anchoringboundedlyrationalexpectations.pdf)). In hetlearn, the cyclical block alone is CD with a common multiplier on every forward term (easy). The full model adds the value-function intercepts as extra linear states, with no per-quarter re-solve and an unchanged Kalman filter. That is medium for three equations, but hard for SW07, where continuation values must be derived for consumption, capital and Tobin's q, investment, price setters and wage setters. Mixing FHP agents with RE or RLS types also requires re-deriving the aggregation, which relies on the geometric horizon distribution.

### Infinite-horizon learning is a model rewrite for SW07

Preston's infinite-horizon learning replaces one-step Euler equations by decision rules that depend on discounted sums of subjective forecasts derived from each agent's budget constraint and pricing problem. The references are Preston (2005), *IJCB* 1(2); Eusepi and Preston (2011), "Expectations, Learning, and Business Cycle Fluctuations", *AER* 101(6): 2844–72; and Milani (2006), *IJCB* 2(3), which estimates an IH-learning model and finds that learning can make mechanical persistence superfluous ([Meggiorini-Milani references](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp9039.pdf); [IJCB](https://www.ijcb.org/journal/ijcb06q3a3.htm)). With CD, the IH IS curve is `x_t = E_tΣ_{T≥t}(βM)^{T−t}[(1−β)x_{T+1} − σ(i_T − π_{T+1} − r^n_T)]`. With a VAR(1) PLM the sums have closed forms, for example `Σ_T(βM)^{T−t}E_{t−1}Y_{T+1} = (I−φ_1)^{-1}[(1−βM)^{-1}I − φ_1²(I−βMφ_1)^{-1}]φ_0 + (I−βMφ_1)^{-1}φ_1²Y_{t−1}`. On US data 1960–2007, IH learning needs **m = 0.20 and a gain of 0.0454, and fits worse than Euler-equation learning** (log ML −277.08 against −267.16; RE −276.20) ([Meggiorini-Milani](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp9039.pdf)). The numerics would reuse hetlearn's PLM, RLS and per-quarter evaluation, and convergence needs the spectral radius of βMφ_1 below one, so the projection facility binds more often. The obstacle is the derivation. Mechanically iterating the Euler-equation forms does not reproduce Preston's equations, so the IH consumption function with habit, the investment and Q block, and Calvo prices and wages with indexation must be derived by hand. That is medium for three equations and hard for SW07. Massaro (2013, *JEDC* 37(3): 680–692) gives the heterogeneous-expectations version, in which aggregate dynamics depend on long-horizon forecasts ([UvA](https://dare.uva.nl/id/b47af7f2-7195-4f4e-ac43-a5f34639b52b)).

### Heuristic switching beyond two types has no Bayesian medium-scale estimate yet

The Brock-Hommes logit extends directly to K types (Brock and Hommes 1997, *Econometrica* 65(5): 1059–1095). The canonical four-type case is Anufriev and Hommes (2012), *AEJ: Microeconomics* 4(4): 35–64 ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aejmic/v4y2012i4p35-64.html)). Its rules are adaptive `p^e_{t+1} = 0.65p_{t−1} + 0.35p^e_t`, weak trend `p_{t−1} + 0.4(p_{t−1} − p_{t−2})`, strong trend `p_{t−1} + 1.3(p_{t−1} − p_{t−2})`, and anchoring-and-adjustment `0.5(p^av_{t−1} + p_{t−1}) + (p_{t−1} − p_{t−2})`. Fitness is `U_{t,h} = −(p_t − p^e_{t,h})² + ηU_{t−1,h}`, and shares follow **`n_{t,h} = δn_{t−1,h} + (1−δ)exp(βU_{t−1,h})/Z_{t−1}`**, with β = 0.4, η = 0.7 and inertia δ = 0.9 fixed across lab groups. No fitting was done ([KER companion](https://maxapress.com/data/article/ker/preview/pdf/S0269888912000161.pdf)). The estimated macro versions are mostly two-type. Cornea-Madeira, Hommes and Massaro (2019, *JBES* 37(2): 288–300) estimate a US Phillips curve by NLS with VAR fundamentalists and naive agents. They find **intensity 5.04 (s.e. 1.64), a mean fundamentalist share of 0.35**, and a BIC of −1690 against −1681 for fixed estimated weights ([accepted manuscript](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/116520/1/CHM_JBES_March_2017.pdf)). Elias (2022), *Macroeconomic Dynamics* 26(4): 920–944, estimates a three-type Euler-equation learning NK model with Bayesian methods on US data and finds it beats homogeneous learning ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/macdyn/v26y2022i4p920-944_3.html)). Branch (2004), *EJ* 114(497): 592–621, estimates naive, adaptive and VAR predictors on Michigan microdata by ML and finds that shares vary inversely with MSE ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecj/econjl/v114y2004i497p592-621.html)). Hommes and Lustenhouwer (2019), *JME* 107: 48–62, use fundamentalists who believe the target and naive agents, read the fundamentalist share as credibility, find an admissible policy region strictly larger than under RE, and get deflationary spirals at the ZLB ([UvA](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/44671470/1_s2.0_S0304393219300170_main.pdf)). Branch and McGough (2010, *JEDC* 34: 1492–1508) pair a costly rational predictor with the adaptive `θ²x_{t−1}`, which is hetlearn's AR(1) without constant, held fixed, with t−1 information ([PDF](https://www.economics.uci.edu/files/docs/faculty_review/jedc_2010_nkdyn.pdf)). **I found no Bayesian estimate of an N > 2 switching model in a medium-scale DSGE.** Published intensities are not comparable across papers either. CHM's 5.04 uses a four-quarter average squared error; KOZ's 0.51 uses an exponentially weighted error; AH's 0.4 uses an unnormalized discounted sum, which is 1/(1−η) times larger than a weighted mean. hetlearn should fix one normalization and document the translation.

### SAC and BLE learning give the best SW07 fits on record

In a behavioral learning equilibrium, agents use univariate AR(1) rules whose mean and first-order autocorrelation match the realized process. SAC learning tracks exactly those moments. The references are Hommes and Sorger (1998), *Macroeconomic Dynamics* 2(3): 287–321; Hommes and Zhu (2014), *JET* 150: 778–814; and Hommes, Mavromatis, Özden and Zhu (2023), "Behavioral learning equilibria in New Keynesian models", *Quantitative Economics* 14(4): 1401–1445 ([QE](https://doi.org/10.3982/QE1533); [BoC SWP 2022-42](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/swp2022-42.pdf)). SAC updates the mean `α_t = α_{t−1} + (1/(t+1))(x_t − α_{t−1})`, the second moment R_t, and the lag-one autocorrelation β_t, which stays in [−1, 1] by construction. The constant-gain version swaps 1/(t+1) for κ, and with t−1 timing the forecast is `α_{t−1} + β²_{t−1}(x_{t−1} − α_{t−1})`. On US SW07 data 1966Q1–2007Q4 (CPI inflation), the log10 Bayes factors against RE are **6.87 for BLE, 9.30 for SAC, 9.01 for VAR(1) learning, 5.33 for AR(2) and 4.72 for pseudo-MSV**. Constant gains run from 0.006 to 0.024. Investment adjustment costs fall from 5.68 under RE to 2.12 (BLE) and 1.38 (SAC), and price indexation rises from 0.20 to 0.50 (BLE). SAC fits best once SPF inflation expectations are added ([BoC SWP 2022-42](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/swp2022-42.pdf); [QE abstract](https://doi.org/10.3982/QE1533)). The ranking says that knowing each variable's mean and persistence matters more than knowing the cross-equation structure, which supports hetlearn's univariate PLMs. SAC is a new update rule for the AR(1) PLM, with state (α, β, R) per variable, and needs no projection facility (easy). BLE is a fixed point of the AR(1) coefficients per parameter draw, and the HMOZ iteration screens out E-unstable equilibria as a by-product (medium). The same routine covers restricted-perceptions and consistent-expectations equilibria, including Lansing's (2009, *RED* 12(2): 304–326) optimal-Kalman-gain CEE; only the moment condition changes.

### Anchoring gains and intercept-only learning connect hetlearn to the unanchoring literature

Four endogenous-gain rules exist. **Threshold switching** between a decreasing gain and a constant gain comes from Marcet and Nicolini (2003), *AER* 93: 1476–1498. Milani (2014), "Learning and time-varying macroeconomic volatility", *JEDC* 47: 94–114, estimates it with Bayesian methods on US data. The switch fires when the mean absolute error over a J-quarter window exceeds a recursive threshold, and the constant gains are **0.082 for inflation and 0.073 for output** (J ≈ 4). Agents sit on the high gain through most of the 1970s and early 1980s ([Milani WP](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~fmilani/MacroVola_stls2.pdf)). **Model-consistency switching** is Carvalho, Eusepi, Moench and Preston (2023), "Anchored inflation expectations", *AEJ: Macro* 15(1): 1–47. The criterion is the distance between subjective and model-consistent coefficients, estimated on US inflation and short-term professional forecasts only, and it predicts long-term expectations out of sample ([AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/mac.20200080)). Gáti reports their unanchored gain as 0.145 ([ECB WP 2685](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp2685~95e6d7b379.en.pdf)). A **smooth anchoring function** `k_t = g(f_{t|t−1})` is Gáti (2023), *JME* 140: S37–S47 ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/moneco/v140y2023isps37-s47.html)). Only the inflation intercept is learned, `π̄_t = π̄_{t−1} + k_tf_{t|t−1}`, with RE slopes. Five knots estimated by SMM on 1981Q3–2020Q1 data, including SPF 12-month CPI forecasts, give **(0.82, 0.61, 0, 0.33, 0.45)**. A −4 pp surprise moves long-run expectations by about 3.2 pp, against 0.1 pp under a constant gain of 0.05. The **adaptive step size** of Kostyshyna-Özden-Zhang is already `gain.rule = 'koz'`. In hetlearn's gain-rule slot the threshold and smooth rules are easy. CEMP's criterion is medium; it needs the current ALM, which the per-quarter re-solve already provides. With t−1 information all four keep the model conditionally linear. CEMP's period-t timing with a nonlinear gain is what forced them onto a particle filter ([HMOZ](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/swp2022-42.pdf)). Gáti's intercept-only learner is worth adding as a PLM: anchored RE dynamics with a drifting long-run mean, called "the smallest possible deviation from rational expectations that has enough flexibility to study the unanchoring of inflation expectations". With a fixed mean at target, the same PLM becomes the Hommes-Lustenhouwer fundamentalist or the De Grauwe targeter.

Survey discipline moves the gains. Estimated US constant gains cluster around 0.006–0.03: 0.0196 in Milani (2011, *EJ* 121(552): 379–401) and about 0.031 in Slobodyan and Wouters' Kalman-filter learning ([Milani 2011](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~fmilani/ESL.pdf); [SW draft](https://www.frbsf.org/slobodyan_wouters_rev.pdf)). When the inflation PLM must also match the SPF, Ormeño's posterior gain is 0.14–0.19 ([CESifo WP 3552](https://www.cesifo.org/DocDL/cesifo1_wp3552.pdf)). Slobodyan and Wouters (2012a, *AEJ: Macro* 4(2): 65–101) find that **AR(2) rules under Kalman-filter learning, or an equal-weight average of five small models, fit SW07 best**. Learning only the MSV parameters (2012b, *JEDC* 36(1): 26–46) fits about as well as RE, with the gains coming from initial beliefs ([openICPSR](https://www.openicpsr.org/openicpsr/project/114246/version/V1/view); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/dyncon/v36y2012i1p26-46.html)). Their 2007 simulations show what high gains cost: at gains of 0.01, 0.02 and 0.05 the projection facility discards **0.04%, 0.37% and 13.8%** of observations ([2007 draft](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/conferences/learningconf/2007/Slobodyan.pdf)). Gains above about 0.05 therefore need a bounded PLM, such as SAC or mean-only.

### Experience-based learning is a fixed gain of 0.018 in aggregate

Malmendier and Nagel (2016), "Learning from inflation experiences", *QJE* 131(1): 53–87, give each cohort a gain that decreases in age, `γ_{t,s} = θ/(t−s)` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjv037)). On Michigan data the 2012 draft estimates **θ = 3.006 (0.249)**. The constant gain that best matches the average weights is **0.0175 per quarter**, "quantitatively very similar" to Milani's estimates ([draft](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~webfac/obstfeld/ulrike.pdf)). Nagel and Xu (2022), *RFS* 35(5): 2190–2245, adopt ν = 0.018 as a fading-memory gain disciplined by microdata ([NBER WP 26255](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26255.pdf)). For SW07 the aggregate version is a mean-only or AR(1) PLM with the gain fixed at 0.018 (easy), and fixing the gain externally removes a parameter from estimation. A true cohort version needs one type per age bin, deterministic age-dependent gains and demographic weights (hard, N types). I found no DSGE that uses one.

### Robustness and ambiguity produce wedges whose loadings come from the model

Hansen-Sargent robustness distorts forecasts toward a worst case that depends on the agent's value function. The references are Hansen and Sargent (2001), "Robust Control and Model Uncertainty", *AER* 91(2): 60–66 ([listing](https://larspeterhansen.org/research/papers/page/8/)), and the book *Robustness* (Princeton University Press; cited as 2007 or 2008). In the LQ case the worst-case shock mean is linear in the state, `w_{t+1} = K̂x_t` with **`K̂ = (θI − C'P̂C)^{-1}C'P̂(A − BF̂)`**, so the robust forecast is `E^0_t x_{t+1} + CK̂x_t` ([QuantEcon](https://python-advanced.quantecon.org/robustness.html)). Under a first-order perturbation the value function is linear, so a constant θ only shifts constants. Bhandari, Borovička and Ho get time variation by making θ_t a state rescaled with shock volatility ([QuantEcon BBH lecture](https://python-advanced.quantecon.org/subjective_beliefs_business_cycles.html)). The US applications I found are Hansen, Sargent and Tallarini (1999), *REStud* 66(4): 873–907, estimated on consumption and investment, and Szőke (2022), "Estimating robustness", *JET* 199: 105225, whose implied interest-rate forecasts reproduce the bias in survey expectations ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/restud/v66y1999i4p873-907..html); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jetheo/v199y2022ics0022053121000429.html)). I found no estimated SW-type model with multiplier robustness. For private agents in hetlearn this is hard: it needs their quadratic objective and a Riccati iteration with `D(P) = P + PC(θI − C'PC)^{-1}C'P` every quarter. A robust central bank is medium, since Giordani and Söderlind (2004, *JEDC* 28(12): 2367–2397) supply MATLAB code ([abstract](https://swopec.hhs.se/hastef/abs/hastef0499.htm)). It distorts the policymaker's forecasts, not private expectations.

Ambiguity aversion à la Ilut and Schneider (2014), "Ambiguous Business Cycles", *AER* 104(8): 2368–2399, is the linear-friendly member of the family ([Duke](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/items/2f8072fe-092c-4efa-9ead-2256ad5ac4b3)). Agents act on the worst-case belief `log Z_{t+1} = ρ_z log Z_t + σ_zε_{t+1} − a_t`, where the confidence process a_t is AR(1). Changes in ambiguity "work like changes in conditional means", so the model "can still be solved and estimated using first-order approximations" ([2012 draft](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f59669/f59669.pdf)). The published abstract reports that TFP and confidence shocks explain **about two-thirds of business-cycle variation, with confidence shocks about 70% of that**. The 2012 draft's mode has agents acting as if next-quarter TFP is 0.435% lower. The hetlearn version (derived) is a wedge with a model-implied loading, `E^amb_t x_{t+1} = E^type_t x_{t+1} − ν_Za_t`, where ν_Z is the impact column of the TFP innovation in the current solution. The per-quarter re-solve updates that loading at no extra cost, and the SPF interquartile range can enter as an observable of a_t (medium). The same template covers ambiguity about the policy shock, as in Masolo and Monti (2021), *JEEA* 19(2): 839–871, who use Blue Chip data to explain long-run inflation expectations ([publicatt](https://publicatt.unicatt.it/handle/10807/227728)). Endogenous ambiguity from learning (Ilut and Saijo 2021, *JME* 117: 354–376) and asymmetric ambiguity are not linear wedges ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22958)).

### Belief wedges and sentiment shocks are additive terms that surveys identify

Bhandari, Borovička and Ho (2025), "Survey Data and Subjective Beliefs in Business Cycle Models", *REStud* 92(3): 1375–1437, are the structural template ([REStud](https://www.restud.com/survey-data-and-subjective-beliefs-in-business-cycle-models/)). Michigan forecasts minus VAR forecasts are positive on average for unemployment and inflation, countercyclical, and close to one-factor: the first principal component explains 76% of wedge variation in the WP and 78.6% in the published version as replicated by QuantEcon. The mean inflation wedge is about **1.2 pp**. In the model, the one-step wedge on any variable is `Δ_t(z) = −θ_tz̄'ψ_wψ_w'v_x'`, driven by a pessimism state θ_t calibrated to the wedges (ρ_θ = 0.714, θ̄ = 5.64), not estimated by likelihood. Without θ_t shocks, unemployment volatility falls to 0.55 against 1.65 in the data ([Richmond Fed WP 19-14](https://www.richmondfed.org/-/media/RichmondFedOrg/publications/research/working_papers/2019/wp19-14.pdf); [QuantEcon](https://python-advanced.quantecon.org/subjective_beliefs_business_cycles.html)). Bianchi, Ludvigson and Ma (2022), "Belief Distortions and Macroeconomic Fluctuations", *AER* 112(7): 2269–2315, measure distortions as survey forecasts minus a real-time elastic-net forecast. Median inflation forecasts are biased upward in every survey, and in 2013:II–2018:II the median SPF growth forecast was biased up by 38% of actual growth ([AER PDF](https://www.sydneyludvigson.com/s/biases_aer.pdf)).

Milani's expectation and sentiment shocks are the estimated reduced form. In Milani (2011), "Expectation Shocks and Learning as Drivers of the Business Cycle", *EJ* 121(552): 379–401, observed SPF expectations equal the constant-gain learning forecast plus AR shocks. **Output-expectation shocks explain 53–54% of output variance at 12–20 quarters** ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecj/econjl/v121y2011i552p379-401.html); [author PDF](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~fmilani/ESL.pdf)). Milani (2017), "Sentiment and the U.S. business cycle", *JEDC* 82, puts the same structure into an SW07-type model with a VAR PLM on the MSV variables, t−1 information and SPF one-quarter-ahead forecasts of consumption, investment and the GDP deflator: `Ê_{t−1}Y_{t+1} = (I + b̂)â + b̂²Y_{t−1} + dα_t` ([Cambridge citation list](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/1FFCA6077C5FE17EFC151947B6BBB292); [revised WP](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~fmilani/ASBCr.pdf)). Sentiment explains over 40% of output and consumption variability and about 60% of investment and inflation. The marginal data density is 2,769 against 2,148 for RE with the same survey observables, the gain is 0.013, and investment adjustment costs fall from 5.96 to 2.67. Angeletos, Collard and Dellas (2018), "Quantifying Confidence", *Econometrica* 86(5): 1689–1726, discipline the wedge differently. Their confidence state ξ_t biases higher-order beliefs about others' current actions, and the solution `X_t = Θ_XX^b_t + Θ_ss_t + Θ_ξξ_t` is a transformation of the RE solution. It explains **51% of output at 6–32 quarters** (sticky prices, US 1960Q1–2007Q4, ρ_ξ = 0.83) ([Econometric Society](https://www.econometricsociety.org/doi/10.3982/ECTA13079); [NBER WP 20807](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20807.pdf)). Noise and news models (Lorenzoni 2009; Blanchard, L'Huillier and Lorenzoni 2013; Barsky and Sims 2012; Beaudry and Portier 2006) and sentiment equilibria (Angeletos and La'O 2013; Benhabib, Wang and Wen 2015) are RE information or equilibrium structures, not expectation rules. hetlearn can only mimic them with wedges.

All of these fit one general form (derived): `x_exp1_t = Σ_i n_{i,t}(E^i_t x_{t+1} + w^i_t)`, with `w^i_t = μ_i + Λ_{i,t}f_t` and `f_t = Rf_{t−1} + η_t`. Milani is Λ = a selection matrix. Reduced-form BBH is one factor with free loadings (easy). Ilut-Schneider is Λ_t = −(TFP impact column of the current solution) (medium). Structural BBH needs v_x, the slope of lifetime utility in SW07 variables, plus BBH's modified Blanchard-Kahn fixed point every quarter (hard). Two design rules follow. Belief distortions with a structural reading should apply to every forward-looking variable from one distorted law of motion; independent per-variable wedges lose the cross-equation restrictions that ACD stress. And because model-consistent RE agents anticipate others' biased expectations while anchored ones do not, the RE variant changes how sentiment propagates. That should be an explicit switch.

De Grauwe's animal spirits are two biased heuristics plus switching, in De Grauwe (2011), "Animal spirits and monetary policy", *Economic Theory* 47(2–3): 423–457 ([LSE](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/45124)). Optimists forecast `g_t` and pessimists `−g_t`, with `g_t = (β + δ·var(y_t))/2`, and for inflation, targeters forecast π* while extrapolators forecast π_{t−1} ([CESifo WP 2418](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp2418.pdf)). I found no US estimate; Jang and Sacht (2016, *Metroeconomica* 67(1): 76–113) use euro-area SMM with the intensity on a grid ([Jang-Sacht](https://www.econ.nagoya-cu.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Jang_Sacht_Animal_Spirits.pdf)). In hetlearn these are fixed-rule types that need N types. A symmetric pair has zero mean bias when shares balance, so matching the upward survey biases needs asymmetric biases or a constant wedge. Overconfidence in the Broer-Kohlhas sense (*REStat* 106(5), 2024: 1334–1351) concerns private-signal precision and needs dispersed information ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/restat/v106y2024i5p1334-1351.html)).

### The rest of the sweep is mostly citations, special cases or nonlinear mechanisms

Several items need no new code. Kreps-style anticipated utility is the standard justification for re-solving every quarter under current beliefs. Cogley and Sargent (2008), *IER* 49(1): 185–221, benchmark it against fully Bayesian decisions ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ier/iecrev/v49y2008i1p185-221.html)), and Milani (2017) invokes it in an estimated SW-type model. Bayesian model averaging is a special case of hetlearn's switching (derived): with Gaussian errors, posterior weights are proportional to `exp(−SSE_i/(2σ_i²))`, a logit in cumulative squared errors with memory one and intensity 1/(2σ²). Evans, Honkapohja, Sargent and Williams (2013) and Cho and Kasa (2017, *AER* 107(11): 3589–3616) show that such averaging can select a non-RE model in self-referential economies ([WP](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~nwilliam/learnbayes.pdf); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v107y2017i11p3589-3616.html)). Gabaix and Laibson's as-if discounting from noisy simulation gives a hyperbolic horizon profile, `D(t) = 1/(1 + αt)`, which is the CD module with a non-geometric profile ([NBER WP 23254](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23254.pdf)). Woodford (2013), *Annual Review of Economics* 5: 303–346, is the citation for the temporary-equilibrium framing that hetlearn already implements ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/anr/reveco/v5y2013p303-346.html)).

A few items are medium-sized. Eusepi and Preston (2010), *AEJ: Macro* 2(3): 235–271, show that communicating the policy rule restores stability under learning; in hetlearn that means a PLM that includes the rule's variables ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/aea/aejmac/v2y2010i3p235-71.html)). Molavi's "Simple Models and Biased Forecasts" restricts agents to the best state-space model with at most d states, which needs a latent-state (Kalman) learner rather than RLS ([arXiv 2202.06921](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2202.06921)). The Farmer-Nicolò belief function, `E_tΔn_{t+1} = Δn_t` for nominal GDP growth, replaces the Phillips curve. It beats the NK model in Bayesian comparisons on US data, and a follow-up for the US, UK and Canada finds a margin of more than 40 log points ([VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/blogs-and-reviews/keynesian-economics-without-phillips-curve); [CEPR DP 13655](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/DP13655.pdf)). As a naive type it is easy; as a replacement equation it is a model change. Text- and machine-learning measures can serve as observables for a wedge: narratives from 10-K filings explain 19% of output variance in Flynn and Sastry ([NBER WP 32602](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32602)), and BLM's distortion series is another candidate. Imprecise memory (da Silveira, Sung and Woodford, [NBER WP 28075](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28075/w28075.pdf)), imperfect problem solving (Ilut and Valchev 2023, *QJE* 138(1): 313–362), tail-risk scarring (Kozlowski, Veldkamp and Venkateswaran, [NBER WP 21719](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21719/w21719.pdf)), Bayesian regime learning (Bianchi and Melosi 2016, *IER* 57(2): 717–756) and LLM-generated expectations are nonlinear, heterogeneous-agent or research-stage mechanisms. They are not near-term candidates for a linear representative-mix toolbox.

## Build survey observables and N types first, then the cheap rules

### Seven cheap additions carry most of the value

The ranking weighs value (published US evidence, a mechanism the toolbox lacks, a clear validation target) against cost in the current architecture. Items 1–7 fit the two-type structure, either as transforms of the RE or BR forecast or as new learner rules. They also gain from the N-type core below. Survey measurement equations should come before all of them, for the reasons given in the identification subsection: items 5 and 7 are identified only through survey observables, and the evidence for items 1 and 4 is strongest with them. The three-equation packages `models/ijcb3` and `models/as07` are the natural test beds for replicating three-equation results before moving to SW07 ([README_PIPELINE.md](../../../README_PIPELINE.md)).

The N-type core needs three regression tests: K = 2 with RE and AR(1) must reproduce the current hetlearn exactly; the four-heuristic simulation with β = 0.4, η = 0.7, δ = 0.9 must reproduce Anufriev-Hommes; and target believers plus naive agents must reproduce the Hommes-Lustenhouwer enlargement of the stable policy region ([AH](https://maxapress.com/data/article/ker/preview/pdf/S0269888912000161.pdf); [HL](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/44671470/1_s2.0_S0304393219300170_main.pdf)). The share rule should fix one fitness normalization and keep a switch for the unnormalized `U_t = −e_t² + ηU_{t−1}` convention, so that published intensities can be reproduced.

### Identification, not mechanics, is the binding constraint

**Survey expectations decide the model comparisons.** In Ormeño's single-author precursor, learning and RE fit macro data about equally (log ML −142.82 against −146.78). With SPF inflation expectations added, the comparison becomes 45.22 against −19.14, **posterior odds of 8.9e27 for learning**, and under RE the survey data cut price indexation from 0.327 to 0.052 ([CESifo WP 3552](https://www.cesifo.org/DocDL/cesifo1_wp3552.pdf)). The published SW07 version, Ormeño and Molnár (2015), *JMCB* 47(4): 673–699, reports the same pattern ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/wly/jmoncb/v47y2015i4p673-699.html)). The diagnostic parameter adds 2.78 log points in SW07 without surveys and 34.67 in a model with SPF data ([LSY](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/wp2023-19.pdf)). SAC learning wins once SPF expectations enter ([QE](https://doi.org/10.3982/QE1533)). Qiu's k, CEMP's anchoring, Melosi's dispersed information and Milani's sentiment are all identified from survey forecasts. The toolbox's own estimate shows the same need: at the learning mode the intensity of choice is 6.67 against the calibrated 0.51, and the project log lists its identification without survey data as open ([README_PIPELINE.md](../../../README_PIPELINE.md)). SPF one-quarter-ahead forecasts map directly into `x_exp1` under t−1 timing, as in Milani (2017). Michigan one-year questions need four-step forecasts from each type's PLM and wedge factor. Survey levels, as opposed to demeaned series, require wedge means, since the average inflation bias is about 1.2 pp ([QuantEcon BBH](https://python-advanced.quantecon.org/subjective_beliefs_business_cycles.html)).

**Expectation parameters trade off against SW07's mechanical frictions.** Angeletos-Huo's anchoring weight of 0.43 is the size usually attributed to indexation. Learning lowers investment adjustment costs (5.68 to 1.38 under SAC; 5.96 to 2.67 in Milani 2017) and habit (0.70 to 0.48 in Milani), and diagnostic expectations do the same in BIS (habit 0.80 against 0.93, adjustment costs 3.30 against 5.24). It also lowers shock persistence: under EE learning with CD, ρ_r falls to 0.23 and ρ_u to 0.07, against about 0.9 under RE ([Meggiorini-Milani](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp9039.pdf)). In a simplified three-equation BNK, Andrade et al. find nine structural parameters mapping into six identified quantities ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.07601)). From macro data alone, expect expectation parameters and frictions to be jointly, and weakly, identified.

**The dampening devices are close substitutes at one step.** Level-k, cognitive discounting and dogmatic higher-order doubts all imply average expectations of the form `d·E_t[y_{t+1}]` ([AHS](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27308/w27308.pdf)). Stacking them on noisy or sticky information double-counts unless multi-horizon surveys separate the profiles: m^h for CD, flat λ for shallow reasoning, rising μ_k for incomplete information with learning. DE pushes the other way from CD, so a combination `m[(1+θ)E_t − θE_{t−1}]` is identified only through dynamics, impact against lagged responses. Within the mix, fixed shares make `n1 + n2·m` a single multiplier, and the level-k depth and the learning gain both govern how fast beliefs respond.

**Selection by squared errors removes some types by construction.** Against a model-consistent RE type, a CD or DE forecast has larger expected squared error, so switching drives it out unless types carry costs or predispositions (Branch-McGough's C). With K > 2 types, the intensity, memory, inertia and costs will be weakly identified. The practical answer is informative priors, one intensity per share group, and fixed costs.

**Timing and sample choice matter.** t−1 information keeps learning models conditionally linear, so the standard Kalman filter evaluates the likelihood ([HMOZ](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/swp2022-42.pdf)). Endogenous gains should therefore be restricted to t−1 information or come with a warning. The consensus under-reaction coefficient for SPF inflation falls from 1.53 over 1968–2017 to 0.29 after 1984 ([AHS](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27308/w27308.pdf)), and hetlearn's 1966Q1–2026Q2 sample spans both regimes. Time-varying shares or gains, or split samples, keep the two regimes from being averaged. Gain priors should keep most mass below about 0.05 unless the PLM is bounded, given the facility rates Slobodyan and Wouters report.

## Conclusion

hetlearn's RE/BR mix is the two-level special case of the Evans-Gibbs-McGough unified model: level 0 is the learner, the anchored RE type is the limit of infinitely many levels when the iteration converges, and shares switch on performance. The per-quarter solve that turns beliefs into a law of motion is the T-map that generates every finite level in between. Level-k, cognitive hierarchy, damped reflection, frozen-status-quo announcement experiments and BLE fixed points are all the same operation, "take an anchor, iterate the model's map, stop somewhere", with different anchors and stopping rules. A continuous depth parameter (Poisson τ, or k by interpolation) gives a one-parameter bridge between the two endpoints of the IJCB structure. The engineering work is bookkeeping plus one new safeguard, an IE-stability check, because finite levels can oscillate where learning still converges.

The mechanics are cheap and the identification is expensive. That changes the order of work, and it changes how to read the literature's numbers. Estimated departures from RE depend on what else is in the model: m is 0.42 when CD is the only departure and 0.94 next to learners. Published estimates are therefore targets conditional on a companion mechanism, not portable priors. On the survey evidence (under-reaction on impact, later overshooting, individual over-reaction after 1984), information frictions combined with diagnostic over-reaction describe measured expectations better than cognitive discounting or level-k do. CD and level-k earn their place as GE dampers in policy counterfactuals. Nobody has estimated an SW07 with level-k, cognitive hierarchy or more than two switching types. A horse race on SW07 with SPF observables, pitting a noisy-information-plus-DE type and a CD or level-k type against the current RE/AR(1) mix, would therefore be new, and it is the test that decides which of these mechanisms the toolbox should keep.
