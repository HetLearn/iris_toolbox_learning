# Cognitive discounting, myopia and planning-horizon distortions of expectations in linear DSGE models

Notation: `m` is Gabaix's micro cognitive-discounting parameter (written m̄ in his paper), `M`, `M^f` are the aggregate coefficients that appear in front of expectations. Hats or "deviation" mean deviation from the deterministic steady state (or from a stated reference point). "WP" marks a working paper. Numbers are as printed in the version cited, which is often a working-paper version; published versions may differ. Research pass dated 2026-10-20.

## Q1. Gabaix (2020): definition of cognitive discounting, the behavioral IS and Phillips curves, implications; Gabaix (2014) sparsity and Gabaix (2019) Handbook chapter

### Takeaway
Cognitive discounting (CD) is a misperceived law of motion for the de-meaned state vector, `X_{t+1} = m(ΓX_t + ε_{t+1})`, so for any variable z with z(0) = 0 the agent forecasts `E^BR_t[z_{t+k}] = m^k E_t[z_{t+k}]`: the discount applies to deviations from the steady state (or a stated default), it compounds with the horizon, and m = 1 is RE. In the baseline it gives `x_t = M E_t x_{t+1} − σ(i_t − E_t π_{t+1} − r^n_t)` and `π_t = βM^f E_t π_{t+1} + κ x_t` with M = m but `M^f = m[θ + (1−θ)(1−βθ)/(1−βθm)] < m`. The multipliers relax the Taylor principle (a peg can be determinate) and damp forward guidance geometrically in the horizon.

Definition
- Assumption 2.1: the agent perceives `X_{t+1} = m G^X(X_t, ε_{t+1})`, m ∈ [0,1], where G^X is the equilibrium transition function and X_t has been de-meaned. Linearized, `X_{t+1} = m(ΓX_t + ε_{t+1})`, so `E^BR_t[X_{t+k}] = m^k Γ^k X_t = m^k E_t[X_{t+k}]` (eq. 10). — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- Lemma 2.2: for any variable z(X_t) with z(0) = 0, `E^BR_t[z(X_{t+k})] = m^k E_t[z(X_{t+k})]`, where E_t is the rational expectation under the true law of motion. So the rule covers endogenous variables as well as exogenous states, as long as they are measured as deviations. — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- Non-zero means (fn. 12): with mean X*, the perceived process is `X_{t+1} = (1−m)X* + m G(X_t, ε_{t+1})`, hence `E^BR_t[X_{t+k} − X*] = m^k E_t[X_{t+k} − X*]`; with trend growth the agent is rational about the balanced growth path and myopic about deviations from it (Online Appendix 12.7). — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- The agent "perceives correctly the average interest rate r̄ and is globally patient with respect to it": `E^BR_t[r̄ + r̂_{t+k}] = r̄ + m^k E_t[r̂_{t+k}]`. Fn. 21: CD "is about the deviation of output from the steady state (and not just the output gap)". Fn. 14: a variant that anchors on present values instead of the steady state (Section 12.3) produces a high MPC. — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- Link to sparsity: CD was "laid out as a possibility" in Gabaix (2016) as a misperception of autocorrelations (fn. 11); Section 9 microfounds it with iterated mental simulations under noisy signals; Section 9.2 endogenizes attention, where "attention affects only the deviations from the default state" (fn. 101), generalizing Gabaix (2014), Definition 1 and Lemma 2 (fn. 102). The paper describes sparsity as tractable because it "uses deterministic models (unlike models with noisy signals) and continuous parameters". — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- Contrast with adaptive learning (fn. 4): CD agents' "mental model never converges to the rational expectations solution". — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)

IS curve
- The subjective Euler equation `E^BR_t[βR_t (c(X_{t+1})/c(X_t))^{−γ}] = 1` linearizes to `ĉ_t = E^BR_t[ĉ_{t+1}] − (1/(γR)) r̂_t = m E_t[ĉ_{t+1}] − σ r̂_t`. Proposition 2.3: `x_t = M E_t[x_{t+1}] − σ(i_t − E_t[π_{t+1}] − r^n_t)`, M = m, σ = 1/(γR). Iterating: `x_t = −σ Σ_{k≥0} M^k E_t[r̂_{t+k} − r̂^n_{t+k}]` (eq. 25), so a rate change k periods ahead is weighted by M^k. — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- Enriched household (Section 6.1): perceived `r̂^BR = m_r r̂`, `ŷ^BR = m_y ŷ`; Lemma 6.1 gives a "term structure of attention" `E^BR_t[r̂^BR(X_{t+k})] = m_r m^k E_t[r̂(X_{t+k})]` (m_r is the intercept, m the slope). Proposition 6.3: `M = m/(R − r̄ m_Y)`, `σ = m_r/(γR(R − r̄ m_Y))`, with `m_Y = (φ m_y + γ)/(φ + γ)`. For a one-parameter model Gabaix recommends m_r = m_y = 1. — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- Fn. 34: if consumers are also inattentive to inflation (parameter m^c_π), E_t π_{t+1} in the IS curve is replaced by `m^c_π E_t π_{t+1}`, and the "1" on the right side of the Taylor criterion becomes m^c_π. In the baseline the expected inflation inside the real rate is not discounted. — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)

Phillips curve
- Lemma 2.4: a resetting firm sets `p*_t = p_t + (1−βθ) Σ_{k≥0} (βθm)^k E_t[π_{t+1} + … + π_{t+k} − μ_{t+k}]`; the firm's extended state includes cumulative inflation since t. Proposition 2.5: `π_t = βM^f E_t[π_{t+1}] + κ x_t`, `M^f = m[θ + (1−βθ)(1−θ)/(1−βθm)]`, and in the baseline `κ = κ̄ = (1/θ − 1)(1−βθ)(γ+φ)`. — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- Separate firm parameter (fn. 15): "If firms had their own rate of cognitive discounting m̄^f, then one would simply replace m̄ by m̄^f in the expression for M^f and in (26)." Firms stay rational about steady-state objects (they discount profits at R = 1/β); βM^f rises with price stickiness θ, unlike the RE coefficient β. — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- Section 6.2 adds firm attention to inflation (m^f_π) and to marginal cost (m^f_x): `p*_t = p_t + (1−βθ)Σ(βθm)^k E_t[m^f_π(π_{t+1}+…+π_{t+k}) − m^f_x μ_{t+k}]` (Prop. 6.4). A value m^f_x < 1 flattens the Phillips curve, and lower m^f_x helps determinacy (fn. 72). — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- Section 6.3 (trend inflation): firms use a "default inflation" `π^d_t = (1−ζ)π̄_t + ζπ̄^CB_t`, where π̄_t and π̄^CB_t are exponentially weighted moving averages of past inflation and of central-bank guidance; non-resetting firms index to π^d. Prop. 6.5: the Phillips curve holds for `π̂_t = π_t − π^d_t`, i.e. `π̂_t = βM^f E_t π̂_{t+1} + κ x_t`, and long-run Fisher neutrality holds. — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)

Calibration and identification
- Table 1: M = 0.85, M^f = 0.80, σ = 0.20, κ = 0.11, β = 0.99; Table 2 ("ancillary"): γ = 5, φ = 1, θ = 0.875, ε = 5.3, m = 0.85. The choice follows Galí and Gertler (1999) (βM^f ≈ 0.8) and Fuhrer and Rudebusch (2004) (IS-curve M ≈ 0.65, s.e. 0.15, called "quite extreme"). M = 0.85 implies an attention half-life of about one year (M^4 ≈ 0.5). — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- Fn. 26: in a simple specification M and the shock variance are not separately identified from the output-gap time series. Fn. 27: the 2018 NBER WP version contains "a tentative Bayesian estimation" that "should be taken as preliminary at best". — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)

Determinacy, forward guidance, other implications
- Proposition 3.1 (rule `i_t = φ_π π_t + φ_x x_t + j_t`): the equilibrium is determinate iff `φ_π + ((1−βM^f)/κ) φ_x + (1−βM^f)(1−M)/(κσ) > 1` (35). With φ_π = φ_x = 0, determinacy iff `(1−βM^f)(1−M)/(κσ) > 1` (36), which never holds when M = 1. Fn. 32: with M = M^f and the Table 1 calibration a peg is determinate iff M < M* ≈ 0.86; dividing κσ by 10 gives M* ≈ 0.95. — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- Listed consequences: lump-sum transfers and "helicopter drops" are powerful because Ricardian equivalence fails; ZLB recessions are bounded and less costly; the forward-guidance puzzle disappears; with behavioral firms optimal commitment no longer calls for price-level or nominal-GDP targeting; the model is neo-Fisherian in the long run and Keynesian in the short run. — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)
- Gabaix notes that the consequences "mostly depend on the reduced form of Proposition 2.5" and "would apply to other models when they have a similar reduced form (for example, Woodford (2018) and Angeletos and Lian (2018))". He also groups Woodford (2018) with level-k thinking: agents "rational with respect to partial equilibrium effects, but don't quite understand general equilibrium effects". — [Gabaix 2019 ms](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2020/BMBCs20/GabaixPaper2.pdf)

### Inferences
- In linear deviation form the one-step CD forecast is a scalar shrinkage of the RE forecast of the deviation: `E^CD_t x_{t+1} = x̄ + m(E_t x_{t+1} − x̄)`. The coefficients that end up in front of expectations are M (equal to m only in the baseline with m_r = m_y = 1) and βM^f, with M^f below m (0.80 against 0.85 at Gabaix's calibration), because the firm discounts cumulative inflation since the reset date.
- Gabaix's baseline IS curve leaves E_t π_{t+1} inside the real rate undiscounted. A toolbox that substitutes one blended forecast of π_{t+1} into every equation sets Gabaix's consumer inflation attention m^c_π equal to the CD parameter by construction.
- The trend-inflation extension (default inflation built from moving averages) is a direct precedent for discounting toward a learned or time-varying reference point instead of a fixed steady state.

## Q2. Estimated versions: who estimates m (and m^f) on US data, what values, and do the data favour discounting over RE?

### Takeaway
Every US study found prefers CD to RE, but the estimates spread widely: full-system single-m estimates under RE run from 0.16 to 0.68 in three-equation models (a single-equation IS estimate is 0.90), separate household/firm estimates are about M ≈ 0.71-0.86 and M^f ≈ 0.41-0.86, and the medium-scale behavioral Smets-Wouters gives m ≈ 0.62-0.73. The main qualification for the toolbox is Meggiorini and Milani (2021): once Euler-equation adaptive learning replaces RE, the estimated m rises to about 0.94 and the learning-plus-mild-CD model fits best; infinite-horizon learning needs even more discounting (m ≈ 0.20).

### Inferences
- Under RE, estimates of m depend on whether a single m drives both curves (0.16-0.68), on whether M^f is free (firm estimates are consistently lower than household estimates in Ilabaca et al.), on the sample (lower after 1982) and on prior tightness (Hirose et al. and Erceg et al. use tight priors near 0.85 and 0.975, and their posteriors sit close to those priors).
- The NKPC discount is weakly identified (robust set [0.07, 0.95] in Andrade et al.); estimating separate m^h and m^f in a medium-scale model with habit and indexation will need informative priors or extra observables such as survey expectations.
- For hetlearn the closest precedents are Meggiorini & Milani (EE learning + CD, which is the toolbox's architecture with a single learner type) and Meggiorini's behavioral SW (RE + CD in the same SW07 equations). Expect m near 0.9-0.95 when CD is estimated jointly with EE learning, and 0.6-0.75 when RE agents are the only alternative.

## Q3. Angeletos & Huo (2021) "Myopia and Anchoring" and the Angeletos & Lian (2023) handbook chapter

### Takeaway
Angeletos and Huo prove that a beauty-contest economy with dispersed noisy information is observationally equivalent to a representative-agent RE economy obeying `a_t = ϕξ_t + δω_f E_t[a_{t+1}] + ω_b a_{t−1}`, with ω_f < 1 (myopia) and ω_b > 0 (anchoring); both distortions grow with the noise and with the strength of GE feedback. Disciplined by SPF forecast-revision evidence, the inflation application gives ω_f ≈ 0.48-0.52 and ω_b ≈ 0.43-0.46, close to Galí-Gertler-López-Salido hybrid NKPC estimates. The Angeletos-Lian chapter treats incomplete information, level-k (with reflective equilibrium and cognitive hierarchy), heterogeneous priors/shallow reasoning and cognitive discounting as four forms of GE attenuation that differ in how under-reaction varies with the forecast horizon.

### Cited Findings

## Q4. Finite planning horizons: Woodford (2019), Gust-Herbst-López-Salido (2022), Woodford-Xie; nesting RE, value-function learning, state space

### Takeaway
In Woodford's finite-horizon planning (FHP) agents make state-contingent plans k periods ahead and value the end of the plan with a coarse continuation value learned from past data by constant-gain updating. With a geometric distribution of horizons the aggregate model is a NK model in deviations from endogenous trends, with ρ in front of all forward terms (including expected inflation in the real rate), plus a backward-looking trend block driven by the learned value-function intercepts. It nests RE as ρ → 1. GHL estimate ρ ≈ 0.46-0.69 and household value-function gains of 0.06-0.14 (firm gain 0.31 when estimated separately) on US 1966-2007 data; the FHP variants beat the RE NK model by 28-39 log points and an Angeletos-Lian/Gabaix-type model by about 3.5-15.

## Q5. Infinite-horizon learning versus Euler-equation learning: equations, estimates, implementation difficulty

### Takeaway
Under RE the two are equivalent. Under subjective expectations, Preston's infinite-horizon (IH) approach writes decisions as discounted sums of expected future aggregates derived from each agent's plan and budget constraint, while Euler-equation (EE) learning keeps the one-step FOCs with subjective expectations. With a VAR(1) perceived law of motion the IH sums have closed forms, so the computation is easy once the IH equations exist, but deriving those equations is model-specific (consumption functions, Calvo sums for prices and wages, investment), so IH is hard to add generically to an SW07 toolbox. Estimated IH models: Milani (2006, IJCB) and Meggiorini-Milani (2021); in the latter, IH fits about as well as RE but needs m ≈ 0.20, while EE learning fits best with m ≈ 0.94.

### Inferences
- Model changes: EE learning (hetlearn's current design) replaces one-step expectations in the FOCs. IH learning replaces each decision rule by a discounted sum of PLM forecasts of aggregates, with individual variables solved out through the budget constraint or the reset-price problem. Mechanically iterating the EE-form equations forward does not reproduce Preston's forms; for example, iterating `π_t = βE_tπ_{t+1} + κx_t` gives `κΣβ^kE_t x_{t+k}`, whereas the IH NKPC has (αβ)^k weights and a `(1−α)βπ_{T+1}` term.
- Computation: with VAR(1) or per-variable AR(1) PLMs, the sums are matrix or scalar geometric series evaluated each period at current beliefs, which fits the toolbox's per-period re-solve. Convergence needs the spectral radius of βMφ_1 (or αβM^f φ_1) below one, so the projection facility matters more than under EE learning.
- Difficulty: medium for the three-equation model (formulas published), hard for SW07 because IH versions of the consumption function with habit, the investment and Tobin's q block, and Calvo prices and wages with indexation must be derived by hand. CD compounds automatically inside IH sums as (βm)^k.

## Q6. Is "a discount term in front of the forward-looking terms" right for hetlearn? Deviations, m^k compounding, separate household and firm parameters, anchored vs model-consistent RE, combination with learning and level-k

### Takeaway
Broadly yes, and Meggiorini (2023) states it as a proposition for any linearized model, with five qualifications. (1) Discount the deviation from a reference point, not the level. (2) The literal multiplier is m^h for a forecast h periods beyond the information date, so a t−1-dated forecast of x_{t+1} would carry m^2, although published EE-learning work uses m. (3) Household and firm parameters are separate, and under Gabaix's own derivation the Phillips-curve coefficient is βM^f with M^f ≠ m^f; Benchimol-Bounader and Meggiorini dispute that derivation and get βm. (4) Expectations that enter several equations (E_t π_{t+1} in the Euler, Q and price/wage equations) need equation- or agent-specific multipliers if households and firms differ, and Gabaix's baseline IS curve leaves expected inflation in the real rate undiscounted. (5) Gabaix's E_t is the model-consistent expectation of the behavioral economy, so shrinking "anchored" full-RE forecasts is a different model. Combination with adaptive learning is published and estimated; combination with level-k is not, but nothing prevents it.

### Inferences
- Implementation rule for a deviation-form SW07 (constants only in measurement equations): `x_exp = n_1E^RE_t x_{t+1} + n_2 m E^RE_t x_{t+1}`. If any expectation variable has a non-zero model steady state, use `x̄ + m(E x_{t+1} − x̄)`. For learners, choose and document the reference point: the true steady state (Gabaix default; it also pulls the learned intercept toward x̄), the PLM's perceived mean `(I−φ_1)^{−1}φ_0` (a "learned default" in the spirit of Gabaix Section 6.3; it preserves drifting perceived trends), or the last observation (Gabaix fn. 14 variant). Meggiorini & Milani multiply the whole PLM forecast, intercept included, which corresponds to the first choice when the data are demeaned.
- Lagged information: a literal Gabaix agent with t−1 information forecasts x_t with m and x_{t+1} with m^2. The convention in Meggiorini & Milani is a single M. Offer both as an option; with m ≈ 0.9 the difference (0.81 vs 0.9) is not negligible.
- Population mixing: with fixed shares, `n_1 + n_2 m` is a single effective multiplier, so n_2 and m are not separately identified. With discrete-choice switching the effective multiplier `n_{1,t} + n_{2,t}m` varies over time, which can identify both, but there is a catch: if E^RE is the model-consistent conditional mean, a CD forecast has weakly larger expected squared error, so a fitness rule based only on squared errors will drive CD agents out unless RE carries a cost or a predisposition term. Against anchored RE (not the true conditional mean in the heterogeneous economy) or against AR(1) learners, CD can win.
- Anchored vs model-consistent: Gabaix-consistent CD agents shrink the forecast from the current (behavioral, heterogeneous) law of motion, which in hetlearn is the model-consistent option re-solved each quarter. Shrinking anchored full-RE forecasts gives "CD about the RE economy", a different and backward-looking-in-expectations model. In the model-consistent case the solver must check Blanchard-Kahn conditions with the CD multipliers in place; CD enlarges the determinacy region (Gabaix Prop. 3.1), so priors on policy coefficients and m interact.
- Household vs firm vs wage setter: because E_tπ_{t+1} appears in the consumption Euler, the Q equation, the price Phillips curve and the wage equation, separate m^h, m^f (and possibly m^w for wage setters) require equation-specific expectation auxiliaries (for example `pi_exp_hh`, `pi_exp_firm`), not one per variable. Whether expected inflation in the real rate is discounted at all (Gabaix baseline: no; Meggiorini and FHP: yes) should be a switch.
- Phillips-curve mapping: since hetlearn will estimate the coefficient b on E_tπ_{t+1}, both derivations are nested; the implied firm parameter is m^f = b/β under Benchimol-Bounader/Meggiorini, and solves `b/β = m^f[θ + (1−θ)(1−βθ)/(1−βθm^f)]` under Gabaix. In SW07's combined Phillips curve with indexation the Gabaix mapping would have to be re-derived; the Meggiorini route is the one with a published medium-scale estimate.
- Level-k plus CD: two natural designs are (a) shrink each level's forecast of the next-period aggregate by m while iterating the model mapping, or (b) apply CD only to the level-0 anchor. Both multiply dampening factors, so separate identification of k and m from macro data will be weak; the horizon profile of survey forecasts is the most promising discriminating moment.
- Overall: the user's statement holds for the reduced form that hetlearn would estimate, provided the multiplier acts on deviations, is equation-specific where agent types differ, and is applied to model-consistent (not anchored) RE forecasts when the goal is Gabaix's equilibrium concept.

### Gaps
- I did not read Benchimol-Bounader's derivation itself; the disputed-derivation claim is reported from Meggiorini's dissertation.
- No paper was found that embeds CD agents in a discrete-choice switching model with heterogeneous shares, or that combines CD with level-k.
- Whether the m versus m^2 choice for lagged information matters empirically has not been studied in the sources found.

## Q7. Mapping the horizon and discounting families into hetlearn: difficulty, reused components, combinations

### Takeaway
Cognitive discounting and its relatives (shallow reasoning, the cyclical block of FHP, the AH reduced form) are easy additions: they reuse the generated-expectation term with a multiplier, the lagged-information chain for horizon compounding, and the existing RE solver. FHP with value-function learning is medium for three-equation models and hard for SW07, but it needs no per-period re-solving. IH learning is hard for SW07 because its equations must be derived by hand, though its numerical machinery reuses the PLM/RLS code.

### Cited Findings
- FHP is a linear Gaussian state space once the trend block `V_t = (I−Γ)V_{t−1} + ΓΦx_{t−1}` is added (GHL eqs. 15-19). — [FEDS PDF](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2020003pap.pdf)
- IH learning with VAR(1) PLMs has closed-form discounted sums evaluated at current beliefs (Meggiorini & Milani eqs. 15-16) and an ALM with belief-dependent intercept and slope. — [CESifo PDF](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp9039.pdf)
- CD in a medium-scale model can be introduced equation by equation with a multiplier on expectational terms (Meggiorini's proposition; behavioral SW estimated with a Kalman filter and RW-MH). — [dissertation](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/18r6t6qb)
- Shallow reasoning (Angeletos-Sastry) replaces average expectations by `λE_t[·]` with λ constant across horizons; CD uses m^k. — [Angeletos & Lian](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29776/w29776.pdf)
- AH reduced form adds one own lag to a myopic forward term; ω's depend on noise and GE strength. — [AH NBER WP](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24545/w24545.pdf)
- A variable-specific, reference-point-based CD operator has been developed for nonlinear, nonstationary and regime-switching DSGE models (Maih 2026, Norges Bank). — [Norges Bank WP 9/2026](https://www.norges-bank.no/en/news-events/publications/Working-Papers/2026/wp-92026/)
