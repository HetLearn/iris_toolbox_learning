# Information frictions and over/under-reaction in expectations: sticky information, epidemiological updating, noisy/dispersed information, rational inattention, diagnostic and natural expectations (notes for hetlearn)

> Notation used throughout. λ = probability that an agent updates in a quarter (Mankiw-Reis, Carroll convention). Coibion-Gorodnichenko (CG) use λ for the probability of *not* updating; translated where needed. G = Kalman gain. θ = diagnosticity. "Anchored" = forecasts computed with the original model's RE law of motion, s_t = T s_{t-1} + R ε_t, E_t x_{t+1} = k + K s_t (hetlearn `re_variant = 'anchored'`). "Model-consistent" = expectations under the law of motion actually in force. Bibliographic details marked "(from memory)" were not re-verified in this session; they are listed again under Gaps.

## 1. Sticky information (Mankiw-Reis): aggregate formula, solution methods, how RE is nested, estimated updating rates

### Takeaway
The sticky-information (SI) average expectation is a geometric average of current and past full-information forecasts, λ Σ_j (1-λ)^j E_{t-j}[·] (or E_{t-1-j} with an information lag), and collapses to RE at λ = 1. In general equilibrium it needs infinitely many lagged expectations of endogenous variables, which the literature handles either with MA/undetermined-coefficient algorithms (Mankiw-Reis 2007, Wang-Wen 2006, Meyer-Gohde 2010) or by truncation (Trabandt 2007; Verona-Wolters 2014: 16-32 lags, 571-1,795 equations, estimation in Dynare judged infeasible). US estimates: firms update roughly every 1.5 quarters and households/workers every 5-6 quarters (MR 2007); SPF inflation forecasts imply updating every 6-7 months (CG). Against sticky prices the fit is mixed to unfavourable (Kiley 2007, Coibion 2010); a dual-stickiness model fits about as well as the hybrid NKPC (Dupor et al. 2010).

- Verona & Wolters (VW) state that the infinite sum cannot be replaced by recursive equations as in Calvo Phillips curves; the lagged-expectation terms are extra state variables that "are not predetermined, but must be solved for in equilibrium" — [VW](https://www.kielinstitut.de/fileadmin/Dateiverwaltung/IfW-Publications/fis-import/c1268090-6443-4541-bd17-a758caa0ed32-VeronaWolters.pdf)

### Inferences
- Translation between conventions: CG's β = λ_CG/(1-λ_CG) = (1-λ)/λ. With β = 1.19-1.23, λ ≈ 0.45 per quarter (mean time between updates about 2.2 quarters).
- Exact recursion (follows from the definition): F_t x_{t+1} = λ E_t x_{t+1} + (1-λ) F_{t-1} x_{t+1}. The lagged term is the SI average *two-step* forecast made at t-1, which in turn needs a three-step forecast from t-2, and so on. This horizon expansion is why there is no finite recursion in general and why truncation or MA methods are used.
- Collapse under anchored forecasts (derived here, not taken from a source): if SI agents forecast with the fixed anchored law, E_{t-j} x_{t+1} = k + K T^j s_{t-j}, so E^SI_t x_{t+1} = k + K W_t with W_t = λ s_t + (1-λ) T W_{t-1}. With the Mankiw-Reis information lag (E_{t-1-j}): V_t = λ T s_{t-1} + (1-λ) T V_{t-1}. One backward-looking block of dim(s) variables (25 for SW07 in hetlearn) serves every SI variable, with no truncation. This is the same "agents project with the RE law of motion from inherited states" device that Bianchi-Ilut-Saijo use for diagnostic expectations (Section 5).
- Model-consistent truncation in hetlearn would extend the existing E_{t-1} chain: E_{t-j} x_{t+1} = x_REt(j+1){-j}, needing J+1 forward chain variables plus about J(J+1)/2 lag auxiliaries per variable (about 150 per variable at J = 16). That is the same order as VW's 571 equations at T = 16, and it would be re-solved every quarter and carried through the time-varying Kalman filter. Estimation would be slow.
- Standard Mankiw-Reis (2002) SIPC (from memory): π_t = [αλ/(1-λ)] y_t + λ Σ_{j≥0} (1-λ)^j E_{t-1-j}[π_t + α Δy_t]; RE limit λ → 1.
- Placement in SW07: the estimated SI-DSGE evidence (MR 2007) finds more stickiness for households and workers than for firms, and the inflation-equation horse races favour keeping Calvo (Kiley, Coibion, Dupor et al.). SI on household-side forecasts (consumption, Tobin's q, wages) next to Calvo pricing looks more promising than SI on inflation alone.

## 2. Epidemiological / sticky expectations (Carroll): recursive form and estimates

### Takeaway
Households absorb the professional (newspaper) forecast with probability λ each quarter. The exact aggregate is the SI formula applied to a fixed target; if households treat inflation as a random walk it reduces to one-lag partial adjustment, M_t = λ S_t + (1-λ) M_{t-1}. Carroll estimates λ = 0.27 on Michigan vs SPF inflation expectations, 1981q3-2000q2 (λ = 0.25 not rejected). RE (or the professional forecast) is nested at λ = 1.

### Cited Findings
- Exact aggregation, Carroll eq. (24): M_t[π_{t,t+4}] = λ N_t[π_{t,t+4}] + (1-λ) M_{t-1}[π_{t,t+4}] (lagged expectation of the *same* four-quarter window). Under the assumption F_{t-1}[π_{t-1,t+3}] = F_{t-1}[π_{t,t+4}] (eq. 19, random-walk perception), eqs. (22)-(23) give M_t[π_{t,t+4}] = λ N_t[π_{t,t+4}] + (1-λ) M_{t-1}[π_{t-1,t+3}]; the newspaper forecast N_t is proxied by the SPF forecast S_t — [Carroll, "The Epidemiology of Macroeconomic Expectations," NBER WP 8695 (Dec 2001)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w8695)
- Estimates (Michigan vs SPF, 1981q3-2000q2): unrestricted coefficients 0.36 on S_t and 0.66 on M_{t-1}; the restriction that they sum to one is not rejected; restricted λ = 0.27; λ = 0.25 is "easily accepted"; about 32% of households (0.75^4) hold expectations more than a year old; no residual serial correlation (consistent with cointegration) — [Carroll NBER WP 8695](https://www.nber.org/papers/w8695)
- News intensity: the squared Michigan-SPF gap falls when inflation news coverage (front-page stories) rises, significant at 5% over 1981q3-2000q2 — [Carroll NBER WP 8695](https://www.nber.org/papers/w8695)
- The model also explains deviations of household *unemployment* expectations from RE (abstract) — [Carroll NBER WP 8695](https://www.nber.org/papers/w8695)
- Published version: Carroll, C. D. (2003), "Macroeconomic Expectations of Households and Professional Forecasters," *Quarterly Journal of Economics* (listed in the references of [CG NBER WP 16537](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16537)); volume 118(1): 269-298 from memory.
- CG note that Carroll's epidemiological model allows different updating rates across agent types; on 1982-2009 with oil-price instruments they find a revision coefficient of 1.3 for professionals and a smaller but highly significant one for consumers — [CG NBER WP 16537](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16537)
- Branch (2007): Michigan data reject a fixed-structure Carroll model in favour of SI with a time-varying distribution of update dates — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/dyncon/v31y2007i1p245-276.html)

### Inferences
- hetlearn version: one extra BR type with a partial-adjustment rule, x_EPI_t = λ·E^RE_t x_{t+1} + (1-λ)·x_EPI_{t-1}, where the "newspaper" forecast is the anchored RE forecast already in the model (or the model-consistent x{+1}). One lagged variable per expectation, no chain. This is adaptive expectations toward the RE forecast rather than toward realised data.
- The two Carroll forms differ only in the target date of the lagged expectation. For a near-random-walk target (inflation) the gap is small; the exact form is the SI recursion of Section 1 (collapsible under anchoring).
- Two-tier information is natural in the toolbox's population mix: professionals with λ ≈ 0.45 (CG on SPF) and households with λ ≈ 0.25 (Carroll), or a household λ that rises with news or forecast-error size, which is in the spirit of Branch's time-varying updating evidence and could reuse the discrete-choice machinery.

## 3. Noisy and dispersed information: Kalman-gain reduced forms, higher-order beliefs, and estimates

### Takeaway
At the consensus level, noisy information with Kalman gain G gives the same partial-adjustment form as SI: F_t x_{t+h} = G E_t x_{t+h} + (1-G) F_{t-1} x_{t+h}, so the CG coefficient is β = (1-G)/G. SPF inflation gives β ≈ 1.2 and G ≈ 0.45 (less than half weight on news). With endogenous variables, dispersed information brings higher-order expectations (Woodford 2003; Nimark 2008); Melosi (2017) truncates that hierarchy and finds the dispersed-information model beats the perfect-information one by about 15 log points. Angeletos-Huo show the equilibrium is observationally equivalent to an RE model with myopia (forward weight f < 1) and anchoring (backward weight b > 0); calibrated to CG's SPF evidence, the NKPC weights are f ≈ 0.52 and b ≈ 0.43, close to estimated hybrid NKPCs.

### Cited Findings
- CG framework: under SI the average forecast is F_t x_{t+h} = (1-λ_CG) Σ_j λ_CG^j E_{t-j} x_{t+h}, which "implies that the current average forecast is just a weighted average of the previous period's forecast and the current rational expectation"; under noisy information each agent runs a Kalman filter on the state, F_{i,t} x_t = G y_{i,t} + (1-G) F_{i,t-1} x_t, so the average forecast satisfies F_t x_{t+h} = G E_t x_{t+h} + (1-G) F_{t-1} x_{t+h} (AR fundamentals); both imply x_{t+h} - F_t x_{t+h} = β (F_t x_{t+h} - F_{t-1} x_{t+h}) + error, with β depending only on the degree of rigidity — [Coibion & Gorodnichenko, "Information Rigidity and the Expectations Formation Process: A Simple Framework and New Facts," NBER WP 16537](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16537)
- CG SPF inflation estimates (1969-2010): β = 1.23 (s.e. 0.50); implied λ_CG = 0.55, i.e. updating every six to seven months under SI, or weight below one half on new information under noisy information. Splitting the revision into current and lagged forecasts gives coefficients 1.24 and -1.27 (s.e. about 0.51), equal and opposite as the theory predicts — [CG NBER WP 16537](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16537)
- Published AER main OLS estimate (Table 1, column 1): β = 1.193 with standard deviation 0.185, as reported by Angeletos & Huo (2021) — [Angeletos & Huo manuscript](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/manuscript.pdf). Citation from memory: CG, *American Economic Review* 105(8) (2015): 2644-2678.
- State dependence in CG: information rigidity of US professional forecasters fell through the 1970s and early 1980s, reached a minimum around 1983-84 and rose during the Great Moderation; it falls substantially after a few quarters of recession and recovers gradually; central-bank independence is positively related to rigidity; inflation-targeting adoption has a small, insignificant effect — [CG NBER WP 16537](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16537)
- Coibion & Gorodnichenko, "What Can Survey Forecasts Tell Us about Information Rigidities?" *Journal of Political Economy* 120(1) (2012): 116-159: impulse responses of mean forecast errors and disagreement to identified structural shocks across consumers, firms, central bankers and professionals; mean forecasts do not fully adjust on impact; WP abstract: forecast errors have a half-life of roughly six months to a year — [IDEAS (JPE)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jpolec/doi10.1086-665662.html); [NBER WP 14586](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14586)
- Individual noisy-information updating, as written by Angeletos, Huo & Sastry (AHS): E_{i,t}[z_t] = (1-g) E_{i,t-1}[z_t] + g s_{i,t}, with g increasing in signal precision; under rational noisy information K_CG = (1-g)/g, the "structural interpretation given in CG" — [AHS, NBER WP 27308](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27308/w27308.pdf)
- AHS GE as-if representation: the imperfect-expectations economy behaves like a perfect-expectations one with Euler equation c_t = -r_t + f E_t[c_{t+1}] + b c_{t-1} + d_t; noisy perception without over-extrapolation gives myopia (f < 1) and anchoring (b > 0); over-extrapolation alone gives hyperopia (f > 1) and no anchoring — [AHS NBER WP 27308](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27308/w27308.pdf)
- Angeletos & Huo, "Myopia and Anchoring," *American Economic Review* 111(4) (2021): 1166-1200: equivalence between incomplete-information equilibrium effects and myopia plus anchoring; shows how to extract the friction from survey evidence — [AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20191436)
- Angeletos & Huo NKPC application (quarterly, discount 0.99, Calvo 0.6, real-marginal-cost AR(1) 0.95 from the labour share, 1947Q1-2019Q2, which gives 0.97 or 0.92): survey moments map into hybrid-NKPC weights (f, b) = (0.52, 0.43) from CG (K_CG = 1.19, individual coefficient set to 0), (0.48, 0.46) from Bordalo et al. (2020) Table 3 (K_CG = 1.41, individual 0.18), and (0.51, 0.44) from Broer & Kohlhas (K_CG = 1.27, individual -0.19). Galí-Gertler-López-Salido (2005) estimates are (0.618, 0.374), (0.635, 0.349) and (0.738, 0.260); the first two lie inside the CG-consistent range — [Angeletos & Huo manuscript](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/manuscript.pdf)
- Melosi (2017) solution: average higher-order expectations about exogenous states, from order zero to a truncation k, are stacked and assumed to follow a VAR(1) whose matrices are found by guess-and-verify; the alternative fixed-point approach of Maćkowiak-Wiederholt "turns out to be harder to combine with" Metropolis-Hastings; the model builds on Nimark (2008) — [Melosi, "Signaling Effects of Monetary Policy," Chicago Fed WP 2016-14](https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/working-papers/2016/wp2016-14-pdf.pdf?sc_lang=en)

- Citation: Melosi, L., "Signalling Effects of Monetary Policy," *Review of Economic Studies* 84(2) (2017): 853-884 — [SUFE listing](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/343988)
- Melosi, L., "Estimating Models with Dispersed Information," *AEJ: Macroeconomics* 6(1) (2014): 1-31: likelihood evaluation of a DSGE with imperfect common knowledge among firms; it explains persistent real effects of nominal shocks better than Calvo stickiness; firms pay little attention to aggregate nominal conditions — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aejmac/v6y2014i1p1-31.html)
- Angeletos & Lian, "Dampening General Equilibrium: Incomplete Information and Bounded Rationality," NBER WP 29776 (2022, prepared for the *Handbook of Economic Expectations*): removing common knowledge, level-k (and a smooth variant), heterogeneous priors and cognitive discounting all mainly attenuate GE feedback in the short run, giving under- or over-reaction in aggregate outcomes — [NBER WP 29776](https://nber.org/papers/w29776)

### Inferences
- Anchored noisy information in hetlearn (derived here): with a scalar gain, Ŝ_t = G s_t + (1-G) T Ŝ_{t-1} and E^NI_t x_{t+1} = k + K Ŝ_t. This is the anchored SI recursion of Section 1 with λ replaced by G, the state-space counterpart of CG's observational equivalence. With signals y_t = D s_t + noise, the steady-state gain matrix comes from one Riccati solution on the fixed anchored (T, R), computed once per parameter draw because the anchored law does not change with beliefs or shares. RE is nested at G = 1 (noise variance zero).
- The Angeletos-Huo numbers imply that SPF-calibrated noisy information alone produces a backward weight on lagged inflation (b ≈ 0.43) of the size usually attributed to indexation. In SW07, which already has price indexation and consumption habit, an equation-level myopia/anchoring implementation would be weakly identified against ι_p and h from macro data; survey observables are needed.
- Model-consistent dispersed information with endogenous signals (Woodford/Nimark/Melosi) requires the agents' higher-order belief state and a fixed point that changes every quarter when the model is re-solved. Hard in hetlearn.

## 4. Rational inattention in DSGE: reduced forms and implementation difficulty

### Takeaway
Rational inattention (RI) makes the signal structure, and hence the Kalman gain, an optimal choice. In linear-quadratic-Gaussian settings the outcome is filter-type partial adjustment with endogenous noise, but the equilibrium needs an iterative fixed point between attention choices and the law of motion. Maćkowiak-Wiederholt models are calibrated rather than likelihood-estimated; estimated DSGE work uses exogenous-noise or sticky-information stand-ins. For hetlearn: hard in model-consistent form; medium if attention is solved once on the anchored RE law and fed into the noisy-information block.

### Cited Findings
- Maćkowiak & Wiederholt, "Business Cycle Dynamics under Rational Inattention," ECB WP 1331 (April 2011) (working-paper version of the *REStud* article): iterative solution. Guess processes for the profit-maximising price and the relative wage, solve firms' attention problem with the infinite-order lag polynomials parameterised as finite ARMA(p,q) polynomials (orders of 2 found sufficient), aggregate prices, compute implied aggregate dynamics, repeat; households are also inattentive; the model is calibrated to match features of US data — [ECB WP 1331](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecbwp1331.pdf)
- Melosi (2017) on the Maćkowiak-Wiederholt approach: a fixed point over the laws of motion of endogenous variables, harder to automate inside a posterior simulator — [Chicago Fed WP 2016-14](https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/working-papers/2016/wp2016-14-pdf.pdf?sc_lang=en)

## 5. Diagnostic expectations: the linear operator, which law of motion, solution with auxiliary variables, estimates

### Takeaway
Yes on both counts. The linear DE operator is E^θ_t x_{t+1} = E_t x_{t+1} + θ(E_t x_{t+1} - E_{t-1} x_{t+1}). In L'Huillier-Singh-Yoo (LSY) both E's are rational expectations under the true (DE-equilibrium) distribution; the model has a unique RE representation with lagged expectations and is solved with standard methods (solution y_t = P y_{t-1} + Q x_t + R v_t). Bianchi-Ilut-Saijo (BIS) solve a "naive" version in which agents project with the shadow RE law of motion from states inherited from the DE economy (the anchored idea) and allow distant memory, E^r_t = Σ_j α_j E_{t-j}; gensys plus "a few additional lines of code". Estimates: θ = 0.44 in SW07 and 0.43 in JPT without surveys, 0.73 with SPF data (LSY); average 0.6 across SPF series (BGMS, 0.45 for the GDP deflator); 1.97 with memory centred six quarters back (BIS), with θ estimated at zero if J = 1 is imposed.

- Law of iterated expectations with J = 1 (derived here): if, as in LSY, the operator is applied to whole linear combinations including date-t terms, E^θ_t[E^θ_{t+1} z_{t+2}] = (1+θ)E_t z_{t+2} - θE_{t-1} z_{t+2} = E^θ_t z_{t+2}, so one-step recursive forms (Euler equations, recursive Calvo sums) stay consistent. ## 6. Natural expectations and restricted perceptions

### Takeaway
Natural expectations (NE) are forecasts from parsimonious, low-order regressions in growth rates fit to data ("intuitive" expectations), in Fuster-Laibson-Mendel (2010) averaged with RE. When fundamentals are hump-shaped (short-run momentum, long-run partial reversal), NE overstate persistence and miss mean reversion. Fuster-Hebert-Laibson (2012) use an AR(40) truth with AR(p) beliefs and drop the weighting parameter. In toolbox terms this is a restricted perceived law of motion; hetlearn's PLM, RLS and population shares already cover it. Survey evidence is unfavourable on two counts: NE produce no disagreement and no learning (Gorodnichenko's discussion), and BGMS find beliefs *exaggerate* long-run reversals rather than miss them.

### Cited Findings
- Fuster, A., D. Laibson and B. Mendel, "Natural Expectations and Macroeconomic Fluctuations," *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 24(4) (2010): 67-84: a "parsimonious quasi-rational model" between RE and naive intuitive expectations; intuitive expectations come from growth regressions with a limited set of regressors, which in some environments produces excess extrapolation; NE overstate the persistence of shocks; the simulated macro model matches volatile asset prices, predictable equity-return cycles and a negative link between consumption growth and future returns — [AEA abstract page](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.24.4.67)
- Fuster, Hebert & Laibson slides (March 2012): fundamentals hump-shaped; agents use parsimonious high-frequency models fit to data; reasons include over-fitting control (AIC/BIC), myopia, recency, complexity aversion; consequences include extrapolation bias, excess volatility and amplified real cycles; the data-generating process is an AR(40) for earnings growth and beliefs are AR(p), with cases 1 ≤ p ≤ 40 studied — [FRBSF slides](https://www.frbsf.org/05-Fuster-Hebert-Laibsot-Natural_Expectations.pdf)
- Citation: Fuster, Hebert & Laibson, "Natural Expectations, Macroeconomic Dynamics, and Asset Pricing," NBER WP 17301 (2011), published in *NBER Macroeconomics Annual 2011*, vol. 26 (2012); per a search summary of the WP, it zeroes out the weighting parameter used in FLM (2010) — [NBER WP 17301](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17301)

## 7. Over/under-reaction evidence: which family fits inflation expectations?

### Takeaway
Three facts: consensus forecasts under-react (CG coefficient > 0), individual forecasts often over-react (BGMS coefficient < 0), and aggregate errors can move against current outcomes (Kohlhas-Walther). For SPF inflation all three depend on the sample: consensus under-reaction is strong over 1968-2017 (1.53) but weak after 1984 (0.29); the individual coefficient is positive and insignificant in the full sample but -0.26 after 1984; published estimates of the individual coefficient differ in sign with outlier treatment. AHS find that expectations under-react on impact and overshoot at medium horizons after the main business-cycle shock, which favours dispersed noisy information plus over-extrapolation and gives little support to cognitive discounting or level-k as expectation mechanisms. For inflation: an information friction (noisy or sticky information) for the consensus, combined with an over-reacting component (DE or extrapolation).

### Inferences
- For inflation the consensus evidence points to a noisy/sticky-information friction (G or λ around 0.45 on the full SPF sample, much closer to one after 1984), and the individual evidence points to an over-reacting component whose strength is sample- and outlier-dependent. Neither pure family fits on its own (AHS Corollary 1).
- Sample choice matters for hetlearn estimation (the module uses 1966Q1-2026Q2, FEATURES row 187): the 1970s dominate the full-sample CG coefficient. - A two-type mix in hetlearn (a noisy/sticky-information type with partial adjustment, and a DE or extrapolative-learning type) has the structure AHS favour. Cognitive discounting and level-k are poor *expectation* mechanisms by AHS's test but are GE dampening devices (Angeletos-Lian); in hetlearn they should be identified from survey data before being stacked on information frictions, since Angeletos-Huo's myopia already comes from noisy information.

## 8. Mapping to the hetlearn toolbox: difficulty, reused components, and combinations

### Takeaway
Diagnostic expectations with one-period memory are close to free in both RE variants: the anchored version needs the original model's two-step forecast coefficients, the model-consistent version uses the existing E_{t-1} chain, and both match published solution methods (BIS naive and LSY). Sticky information is not free in model-consistent form (truncation costs grow with the square of the lag length and VW found estimation infeasible in Dynare), but under anchored forecasts it collapses to one backward-looking block of 25 states for SW07, and anchored noisy information is the same block with λ replaced by G. Carroll-type updating and natural expectations are easy (one lag, or the existing PLM). Model-consistent dispersed information and rational inattention are hard. Survey observables (SPF) are needed to identify θ, λ and G separately from habit and indexation.

- Where the friction sits: DE, SI, noisy information or Carroll updating can be applied *to the RE type* (x_exp1 = n1·E^friction + n2·E^BR), which keeps hetlearn's two-type switching unchanged, or added as a third type, which needs multinomial-logit shares over three fitness measures.
- Combination with learning: diagnostic learners use (1+θ)F^PLM_t x_{t+1} - θF^PLM_{t-1} x_{t+1}, where the second term is the PLM's two-step forecast made one period earlier (computable from the stored beliefs). Sticky learners (beliefs refreshed with probability λ) reduce, at the consensus level, to a smaller effective gain plus partial adjustment of forecasts. No estimated DSGE combining DE or SI with adaptive learning was found in the sources read.
- Combination with level-k and cognitive discounting: all three of incomplete information, level-k and cognitive discounting dampen GE effects ([Angeletos-Lian](https://nber.org/papers/w29776)), and AHS show that level-k, cognitive discounting and dogmatic higher-order doubts all imply average expectations of the form d·E_t[y_{t+1}] ([AHS](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27308/w27308.pdf)). Stacking them on noisy or sticky information double-counts dampening unless survey data separate them. DE (θ > 0) works in the opposite direction to cognitive discounting, so a combination m[(1+θ)E_t - θE_{t-1}] is identified only through dynamics (impact vs lagged response).
- Combination with heterogeneous shares: switching on squared errors applies unchanged to any of these types. A DE type makes larger errors after shocks and would lose share in volatile periods, while a noisy-information type with G < 1 makes serially correlated errors; Branch (2004, 2007) is the closest empirical support for switching or time-varying updating on Michigan data.
- Kalman filter: anchored blocks (W, Ŝ, lagged states) are backward-looking states that the time-varying likelihood filter carries at extra cost proportional to their number (25 per block in SW07). Model-consistent truncated sticky information would add hundreds of states per variable and make the per-quarter re-solve plus filtering slow. Adding SPF inflation (and output growth) forecasts as observables with measurement error, as LSY and Melosi do, is the main route to identifying θ, λ and G.
