FIGURE NOTES - hetlearn paper (Computational Economics), SW07 results
Written by paper/figures/make_figures.py from results/summary_v3/*.csv only; do not edit by hand.
Baseline (2026-10-21): published SW07 parameters (1966Q1-2004Q4 mode), AR(1) learners on inflation with the KOZ endogenous gain from 0.05, intensity of choice chi 2, memory 0.8, RE agents and learners on t-1 information, anchored RE agents, filtered RE history, projection facility 'alm', data 1966Q1-2026Q2 with 2020Q1-2020Q3 treated as missing.
Units: inflation, output and consumption growth in % q/q annualised (growth per capita); policy rate in % a year; pp = percentage points. Colours: data black (thin, dots), RE blue dashed, learning vermillion solid; alternative PLMs green dash-dot, purple dotted, orange long dash with open triangles; Ramsey runs dotted with open circles (Okabe-Ito colours).
Each figure is a PDF (vector) and a PNG (600 dpi), 16 cm wide, 8-9 pt type.

fig_app1 - Application 1: forecast from the 2022Q2 inflation peak
Files: paper/figures/fig_app1.pdf, fig_app1.png
- Shows: unconditional forecast (zero future shocks) from the 2022Q2 inflation peak, 2022Q3-2026Q2, for the RE benchmark (a1_re: every agent rational, RE information t-1) and the learning economy (a1_learn: anchored RE agents and AR(1) learners on inflation expectations, switching shares). Panels a-d: inflation, policy rate, output and consumption growth, 2018Q1-2026Q2, with the data; the forecast is shaded and the model lines start at the 2022Q2 value. Panel e: the learners' end-of-quarter share n2 (it is the share in force in the next quarter, n2_model). The data line has a gap in 2020Q1-2020Q3 (covid quarters treated as missing).
Key numbers:
  * Average inflation 2022Q3-2026Q2: learning 3.45%, RE 2.59% (+0.86 pp); data 3.21%.
  * Inflation gap learning - RE peaks at +3.54 pp in 2023Q1; learning inflation falls below RE from 2024Q4 (2026Q2: 1.25% vs 2.02%).
  * Policy rate average 5.42% learning vs 4.53% RE; output growth 3.39% vs 3.69%; consumption growth 1.96% vs 2.27%.
  * Learners' share n2: 0.467 at the end of 2021Q2, 0.591 at the end of 2022Q2; in the forecast it peaks at 0.685 (2023Q1) and is 0.513 in 2026Q2.
  * Learners' AR(1) coefficient at the origin: beta = 1.061 (above 1, so the perceived law of motion is explosive and has no finite perceived mean). The projection facility ('alm') never acts in a1_learn (0 engine events).

fig_history - learners' share and perceived persistence, 1966-2026
Files: paper/figures/fig_history.pdf, fig_history.png
- Shows: the inflation learners' end-of-quarter share n2 (panel a) and the AR(1) coefficient beta of their perceived law of motion (panel b), learned on the RE-filtered history 1966Q1-2026Q2 (scenario a6_smooth_learn, which is a1_learn with the origin moved to 2026Q2; the two agree through 2022Q2, largest difference 0 in n2 and 0 in beta). The dash-dot line is the Application 1 forecast from 2022Q2 with zero shocks (a1_learn). The learners keep learning through the 2020Q1-2020Q3 gap on the filtered values (the RE prediction).
Key numbers:
  * Share n2 over 1966Q1-2022Q2: mean 0.528, min 0.303 (1975Q2), max 0.718 (2013Q3).
  * Share n2: 0.527 at the end of 2019Q4, 0.467 at the end of 2021Q2, 0.591 at the end of 2022Q2, 0.632 at the end of 2026Q2 on the data (zero-shock forecast: 0.513). On the data after 2022Q2 the share falls to 0.229 (2022Q4), its lowest value since 1966Q1, while the zero-shock forecast has it rising to 0.685 (2023Q1): realised inflation fell faster than the learners' AR(1) predicted.
  * beta over 1966Q1-2022Q2: mean 0.747, min 0.274 (2017Q3), max 1.061 (2022Q2); 0.317 in 2019Q4, 1.061 in 2022Q2, 0.763 in 2026Q2 on the data.
  * beta is at or above 1 in 3 quarters of 1966Q1-2026Q2: 1974Q3, 2022Q1, 2022Q2.

fig_app3 - Application 3: policy 100 bp looser or tighter than the rule
Files: paper/figures/fig_app3.pdf, fig_app3.png
- Shows: the policy rate held 100 bp a year below (looser) or above (tighter) the Taylor rule for 4 quarters, 2022Q3-2023Q2 (shaded; the policy-shock process ms is held 1 pp annualised off its own path and the monetary shock em is endogenised to deliver it), from the 2022Q2 peak; deviations from the same scenario without the condition (Application 1), under RE (a3_*_re) and learning (a3_*_learn). Top: inflation and output growth; bottom: looser + tighter, which is zero if the two responses are symmetric. Units: pp of the annualised rates.
Key numbers:
  * Inflation, largest effect: RE +0.666 pp (2023Q3); learning looser +0.717 pp (2024Q1), tighter -0.730 pp (2024Q1). Learning delays the inflation effect and makes it slightly larger.
  * Output growth, largest effect: RE +1.322 pp (2023Q2); learning looser +1.382 pp (2023Q2). Policy rate (including the rule's response): RE -1.735 pp (2023Q2), learning -1.817 pp (2023Q2).
  * Asymmetry under learning, largest |looser + tighter|: inflation -0.0138 pp (2023Q4), about 2% of the effect; output growth +0.0065 pp (2024Q2). Under RE the responses are symmetric to 6.0e-14.
  * The asymmetry comes from the learning states: looser policy raises the learners' share in force by up to 0.0244 (2024Q3), tighter policy lowers it by up to 0.0295 (2024Q2).

fig_app4 - Application 4: Ramsey policy against the Taylor rule
Files: paper/figures/fig_app4.pdf, fig_app4.png
- Shows: Ramsey policy under commitment against the Taylor rule from the 2022Q2 peak, balanced loss weights (inflation 1, output gap 0.5, rate 0.1), zero shocks, 2022Q3-2026Q2; inflation, output gap (y - y^f, the loss target) and policy rate; top row RE, bottom row learning. RE Ramsey = the saved model-consistent RE plan a4_ramsey_balanced_re_mc; learning Ramsey = a4_ramsey_balanced_learn. All application 4 runs use period-t RE information (Ramsey policy is not supported with t-1 RE information), so the rule paths differ from Application 1.
Key numbers:
  * Discounted loss over 80 quarters, balanced weights: RE rule 230.1 vs RE Ramsey 127.7; learning rule 359.4 vs learning Ramsey 184.5.
  * Average inflation 2022Q3-2026Q2: RE rule 2.14%, RE Ramsey 3.39%; learning rule 2.69%, learning Ramsey 4.24%. Average output gap: RE rule -2.79%, RE Ramsey -0.86%; learning rule -4.08%, learning Ramsey -0.77%.
  * Policy rate in 2022Q3: RE rule 2.46%, RE Ramsey 4.40%, learning rule 3.24%, learning Ramsey 10.83% (origin value 0.77%). Ramsey trades a slower fall in inflation for a much smaller output gap under both expectation regimes.
  * Caveat for the text: the anchored RE agents in the learning Ramsey run forecast with the RE solution under the Taylor rule, not under the Ramsey policy, so the learning Ramsey loss is not a clean gain from optimal policy (open design question in the project). Under learning the planner treats the current PLM coefficients and shares as permanent (anticipated utility) and re-optimises every quarter, honouring past multipliers (docs/DESIGN.md). The anchored RE Ramsey runs (a4_ramsey_*_re) are not RE plans and are left out of the figure; their losses are in the notes of tab_app4.

fig_density - Application 5: density forecasts, mark-up and demand shocks
Files: paper/figures/fig_density.pdf, fig_density.png
- Shows: 90% bands (5th-95th percentiles) and medians of 500 stochastic forecasts from the 2022Q2 peak, 2022Q3-2026Q2, with normal draws of the calibrated shock standard deviations (seed 0). Rows: mark-up (supply) shocks, price and wage mark-up (epinf, ew); demand shocks, risk premium, government spending and investment (eb, eg, eqs). Columns: inflation and output growth (% q/q annualised). RE band hatched with dashed edges; learning band filled. The 68% bands are in app5_density.csv, not drawn.
Key numbers:
  * Mark-up shocks, average width of the 90% inflation band: learning 14.59 pp vs RE 4.76 pp (ratio 3.06); in 2026Q2 the learning band runs from -2.04% to 16.62% (RE -0.99% to 4.64%). The learning distribution is skewed upwards: inflation shocks feed the learners' explosive AR(1) beliefs (beta 1.061 at the origin) and their share.
  * Mark-up shocks, output growth band: learning 7.51 pp vs RE 3.02 pp.
  * Demand shocks: the bands are about the same under learning and RE; inflation 1.16 vs 1.18 pp, output growth 10.03 vs 9.92 pp.
  * All 500 draws are used in both cases; the projection facility ('alm') acts in 23 of the 500 learning mark-up draws (46 quarters with beliefs reverted, 13 with shares kept) and in 0 demand draws.

fig_plm - perceived laws of motion: AR(1) against AR(2) and VAR(1) learners
Files: paper/figures/fig_plm.pdf, fig_plm.png
- Shows: Application 1 (forecast from the 2022Q2 peak, zero shocks) with four perceived laws of motion (PLMs) for the inflation learners: the baseline AR(1) (a1_learn), AR(2) (v2_ar2), a VAR(1) in inflation, output gap and policy rate (v2_var3) and a VAR(1) in the 12 forward-looking variables of SW07 (v2_var12). Only the inflation expectation uses the PLM; the other VAR equations only forecast the regressors. Panels: inflation with the data and the RE benchmark; the learners' end-of-quarter share n2; the largest root of the PLM (for the AR(1) the coefficient beta). 2018Q1-2026Q2, forecast shaded.
Key numbers:
  * Average inflation 2022Q3-2026Q2: AR(1), baseline 3.45%; AR(2) 3.69%; VAR(1): inflation, output gap, rate 2.57%; VAR(1): 12 forward-looking variables 3.19%; RE 2.59%.
  * Share n2 at the end of 2022Q2: AR(1), baseline 0.591; AR(2) 0.599; VAR(1): inflation, output gap, rate 0.598; VAR(1): 12 forward-looking variables 0.611. Peak share in the forecast: AR(2) 0.760, AR(1) 0.685, VAR(1) 3 variables 0.659.
  * Largest root at 2022Q2: AR(1), baseline 1.061; AR(2) 1.086; VAR(1): inflation, output gap, rate 1.030; VAR(1): 12 forward-looking variables 1.004 (all at or above 1, so no PLM has a finite perceived mean at the origin).
  * Mean largest root over 1966Q1-2022Q2: AR(1), baseline 0.747; AR(2) 0.806; VAR(1): inflation, output gap, rate 0.953; VAR(1): 12 forward-looking variables 0.998. The VAR learners perceive inflation as close to a unit root throughout the history. No projection-facility events in any of the four runs.
